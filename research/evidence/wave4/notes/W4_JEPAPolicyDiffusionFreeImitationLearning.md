# W4_JEPAPolicyDiffusionFreeImitationLearning — JEPA Policy: Diffusion-Free Imitation Learning via Paired Action and Future Representation Prediction (2026, arXiv 2609.09630)
Setup: Extends MIP ("minimal iterative policy", deterministic 2-pass regression with training noise, Pan et al. ICLR'26). One shared transformer (~20M) processes action tokens + future-representation tokens with full bidirectional attention; target = stop-grad pooled global embedding (same live encoder, no EMA target) of the observation k=4 steps (0.2 s) ahead; target-RMS-normalized MSE, loss ratio ρ=0.10; temporally consistent random crop. Encoder ResNet-18 trained from scratch (~23M); total ~44M params; <6 GiB training memory. Sim: 9 tasks (LIBERO 2 × 50 demos, robomimic 3 × 200, MimicGen 4 × 1000), 20 Hz, H=10/16, execute 8, 3 seeds, 300k steps, 30 evals × 40 episodes, best-checkpoint (plus selection-free checks). Real: two-arm ARX-5, 3 RGB streams at 128×128, actions 10 Hz, 100 demos/task, all methods from scratch, 5 tasks, 3 checkpoints × 10 episodes (630 episodes), RTX 3090, SYNCHRONOUS loop (no overlap).
Claim: jointly predicting action chunk and its paired future latent in one shared stack improves a deterministic few-pass policy, beats Diffusion Policy, at ~13 ms/decision.
Evidence:
 - Sim 9-task mean (Table II): MIP 77.4, Dual-independent (ACT-JEPA-style separate branch) 76.3, Dual-cross 77.6, Diffusion Policy (100-step DDPM, best of abs/delta actions) 75.1, JEPA 83.0. Gains all 9 tasks vs MIP (+5.6, paired t CI [+1.0, +10.2]); largest Coffee Prep 57.5→77.5, Tool Hang 65.8→75.0 (DP 46.7).
 - Selection-free (Table IV): last-checkpoint MIP 62.5 vs JEPA 67.5 (gain persists +4.7–5.6).
 - Latency (Table III, batch 1): MIP 12.9 ms, JEPA 13.2 ms, DP-100 439.5 ms; DP at 50/20/10/4 steps 223/94/47/21.5 ms.
 - REAL (Table XI, pooled episode-weighted, 100 demos/task): DP-100 18.7%, DP-16 31.8%, MIP 54.7%, JEPA 66.9%. Per task (all ckpts): Cabinet 0/0/30/60; Cup Stack 10/32/40/57; Cup Upright 27/40/60/72; Plate Grape 10/10/50/47; Pen Insert 47/77/93/97. Real latency median/p95: JEPA 20.1/63.3 ms, MIP 27.6/67.5, DP-16 79.2/130.8, DP-100 316/373 ms (DP skipped 1/3 expired actions per chunk).
Ablations:
 - Topology: future loss in separate branch (Dual-indep 76.3) or cross-attn (77.6) = no gain; shared stack 83.0. Cutting gradient from future loss to trunk (back-cut) removes gain (73.1 vs MIP 73.8 vs JEPA 82.8, one seed); forward-cut keeps half (78.4).
 - ρ ∈ {0.05,0.10,0.20} and k ∈ {2,4,6}: never materially below baseline; k=4–6 > k=2 (0.1 s horizon too redundant).
 - Camera config (one seed, directional): Standard (fixed+wrist) MIP vs JEPA Moka 90/95, Mug 92.5/95, Square 92.5/97.5, ToolHang 65/65, Coffee 30/70, Kitchen 62.5/77.5, 3-Piece 60/65; WRIST-ONLY baseline much worse (62.5/80/80/27.5/37.5/42.5/15); FIXED-ONLY baseline 87.5/70/90/35/67.5/65/42.5 — both single-camera settings below fixed+wrist on hard tasks (Tool Hang 65 vs 27.5/35; 3-Piece 60 vs 15/42.5).
 - SIGReg isotropic regularizer destroys performance (Moka 92.5→0, Tool Hang 75→2.5).
 - λa=0 (future loss only) → representation collapse (TE32 0.170→0.0014) → action loss is what prevents collapse.
 - Future-prediction error ranks failures (AUROC 0.55–0.75 per task) but must be calibrated per task.
Failure/limitations: real study small (10 episodes/cell, one lab, cell noise 5/10 vs 9/10 on repeats); advantage over MIP in real not significant (+12 pts, p=0.27) but over DP consistent (29/30 cells); DP in sync loop penalized by latency; deterministic predictor averages multimodal futures.
Conflicts: Directly challenges "human demos need a generative head" (ACT CVAE ablation, DP): a DETERMINISTIC 2-pass regressor with training noise (MIP) beats DP-16/DP-100 on real bimanual tasks with 100 demos (54.7 vs 31.8/18.7). Consistent with MIP's thesis (iterative computation + noise injection, not distribution modeling, explains flow-policy gains). Contradicts HybridFlow's DDIM-16 being competitive — but here DP ran synchronously and at 128×128 from scratch. Aux future prediction helps only when in the shared stack — explains mixed results of auxiliary-head papers.
Relevance: Very relevant: small (44M) from-scratch policy, 100 demos, 3 cams incl. wrist, 10 Hz, trains in <6 GiB (fits 8 GB), ~13–20 ms inference. Recipe to try: MIP-style 2-pass deterministic head + shared-stack future-latent loss (ρ=0.1, k≈0.2–0.3 s). Also fixed+wrist > either alone.
Decision impact:
 - Q01 action head: deterministic 2-pass regression with noise (MIP) ≥ Diffusion Policy in real (54.7 vs 31.8 DP-16) and sim (77.4 vs 75.1) — M (real 10 eps/cell, DP sync-latency confound)
 - Q09 auxiliary objectives: future-latent prediction helps ONLY in shared stack (+5.6 sim, +12 real n.s.); separate-head aux gives 0 — M
 - Q10 latency: 13–20 ms two-pass vs 316 ms DP-100; sync DP loses stale actions — M
 - Q11 cameras: fixed+wrist > wrist-only or fixed-only (e.g. Tool Hang 65 vs 27.5/35) — L/M (single seed sim)
 - Q12 model size: 44M from-scratch model, <6 GiB training, beats DP at 100 real demos — M
 - Q03 vision encoder: ResNet-18 from scratch at 128×128 sufficient in-domain (no robustness test) — L
