# W4_MOVEASimpleMotionBasedDataCollectionPara — MOVE: A Simple Motion-Based Data Collection Paradigm for Spatial Generalization in Robotic Manipulation (2025, arXiv 2512.04813)
Setup: Diffusion Policy (DDIM; real: pred horizon 8, action horizon 8, obs horizon 2, 200 epochs, bs 128). Sim: Meta-World 10 tasks with wide randomization of object/target/camera, scripted experts, 3 seeds, data budget in timesteps (5k–100k). Real: Agilex PIPER, RealSense scene camera, ONE task: pick an orange and place on a plastic tray (≈ our pumpkin→tray), human teleop (Pika), training positions from 20 random orange/tray pairs, workspace semicircle r=60 cm; eval 30 unseen positions on a 40×80 cm grid. Real datasets 35k and 75k timesteps (static avg 459 steps/episode, MOVE 549 → ≈76/64 episodes at 35k; ≈163/137 at 75k).
Claim: moving objects (and camera, in sim) during each demonstration injects many spatial configurations per trajectory, improving spatial generalization per unit of collection effort.
Evidence:
 - Static-paradigm degradation (Meta-World Pick-Place, Table I): randomize object only 67.5% → +target 44.7% → +camera (0–π view angle) 31.7%.
 - Preliminary (Pick-Place sim, equal timesteps): sparse 9-point sampling static 29.5% vs MOVE 80.8%; dense sampling 66% vs 74%; circle sampling in-circle 21.3 vs 43.7, out-of-circle 44.6 vs 67.4.
 - Meta-World Table II (equal timesteps, 3 seeds, avg over 10 tasks): Static 22.2%, ADC (reset object mid-trajectory) 27.6%, MOVE 39.1%. E.g. Pick-Place 31.7/47.2/67.3; Window Open 51.2/45.5/70.5. Pick-Place-Wall: MOVE @20k ≈ static @100k (up to 5× data efficiency).
 - REAL (Table III, 30 trials): 35k steps: static 3.3% SR (score 0.055) vs MOVE 23.3% (0.311); 75k: static 23.3% (0.389) vs MOVE 36.7% (0.522). MOVE@35k ≈ static@75k.
Ablations: adding more dynamic dimensions (pick object motion → + target motion/rotation → + camera motion) monotonically improves (figure only); robust to max-velocity hyperparameter across 0.01–0.1 (figure only).
Failure/limitations: authors: camera motion only in sim (real camera fixed); simple tasks. Critical: real = one task, 30 trials, absolute SR low (≤36.7%) even with ~140 demos — DP with small horizons and wide 40×80 cm workspace; human second operator moves objects with transparent tongs (must stay out of view); moving objects produce demos where the robot chases a target — could teach reactive behavior but also non-Markovian cues. Equal-timestep comparison favors MOVE since each MOVE trajectory is longer but fewer episodes.
Conflicts: Consistent with data-diversity-over-count findings (e.g. Data Scaling Laws: environment/object diversity > demos per config) and with sim2real study where spatial/camera randomization matters most. Suggests low SR with ~50–150 static demos on wide-workspace pick-place — consistent with ArmnetBench ~60–70% ceiling on simpler narrow tasks.
Relevance: Directly our task form (fruit → tray, RealSense scene cam, human teleop, ~50–150 demos). Cheap protocol change for our collection: have a helper nudge the pumpkin/tray during some demos (tongs out of view), and randomize positions widely. Also suggests our demo count (50–100) with static placements will give poor spatial coverage. Camera-motion augmentation in sim helped — for us, a handheld/moving scene camera isn't possible, but small camera re-positioning between episodes is (our "moved camera" failure).
Decision impact:
 - Q13 data diversity: supports maximizing spatial configurations per demo (moving objects mid-demo) over more static demos: real 3.3→23.3% at 35k steps, MOVE@35k ≈ static@75k — confidence M (real but 1 task, 30 trials).
 - Q14 robustness (spatial / camera): adding camera variation to static data drops SR 44.7→31.7%; camera motion in demos helps (sim) — confidence L-M.
 - Q06 chunking: DP pred 8 / exec 8 used, not ablated — confidence L.
