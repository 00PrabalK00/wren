# W4_SimulationDrivenImitationLearningforBios — Simulation-Driven Imitation Learning for Biosignals-Free Shared-Autonomy Prosthetic Grasping (2026, arXiv n/a in text)
Setup: Isaac Lab; 200 objects × 10 procedurally generated rooms → 2000 synthetic reach-grasp-lift demos (BoDex-optimized grasps, retargeted reach trajectories), 30 Hz, wrist-camera RGB only (320x240) + 6-D finger proprio; policy controls only fingers (wrist motion given by human). IL baselines: ACT (CVAE, 30-step proprio history, chunk 25), VTM-VAE (Mamba), HannesImitation (diffusion policy, 2 frames, predict 8 execute 4). Sim eval: 5 parallel envs per trial. Real: wearable prosthetic hand, 12 participants × 5 objects × 3 table/wall scenes × 5 trials × 2 methods = 1800 trials.
Claim: sim-generated diverse wrist-view demos yield a prosthetic grasping policy that is robust across real scenes, unlike a policy trained on real data from one scene.
Evidence: Real Stat-B SR: sim-trained ACT-style model 0.92±0.15 vs real-data VTM-VAE 0.66±0.47. Per scene VTM-VAE 0.98 / 0.99 / 0.00 (complete collapse on yellow table with different wall texture; trained only on white-table scenes); ours 0.90 / 0.92 / 0.95. Failures: 32 slip, 25 no closure, 8 pushed away, 3 pose, 1 premature closure.
Ablations (sim, ACT): training objects 10 → 100: unseen-object SR 26.84 → 42.22 (saturates beyond ~75: 42.24 → 42.22). Training rooms 2 → 8: unseen-room SR 44.20 → 57.40. Joint 10 obj/1 room → 100 obj/8 rooms: 21.40 → 42.40. Algorithms (Table II; room / object / both SR): ACT 57.40/42.22/42.40; VTM-VAE 55.75/44.90/44.20; diffusion HannesImitation 48.95/33.24/40.10.
Failure/limitations: finger-only control (wrist driven by human) → simpler than full-arm manipulation; sim SR absolute low (~42–57%); real scenes only 3 table colors; baseline trained on different (real) dataset and architecture — confounded comparison; no augmentation baseline (e.g., color jitter on real data) tested.
Conflicts: the 0% collapse under a table/wall color change for a real-data policy mirrors our lighting/appearance failures and RoboSplat's un-augmented baselines. ACT ≥ diffusion here (small data, simple action) — agrees with some low-data comparisons, contradicts DP-favouring benchmarks; differences are small and settings differ.
Relevance: moderate. Confirms (a) scene-appearance diversity (rooms/backgrounds) is what buys robustness, object-count diversity saturates ~75 objects; (b) a wrist-camera-only policy trained on single-background real data can completely fail on a new table color — wrist cam alone is NOT automatically background-invariant; (c) ACT/CVAE chunking works well in this low-dim, small-data setting.
Decision impact:
 - Q14 robustness: real-data policy from one scene: 0.98 → 0.00 SR on new table/wall color; scene-diverse training 0.90–0.95 across scenes — confidence M (1800 real trials, but confounded methods).
 - Q13 data diversity: room diversity 2→8: +13.2 SR; object diversity 10→100: +15.4, saturating ~75 — confidence M (sim).
 - Q11 cameras: wrist-only view still vulnerable to background/table appearance shift — confidence L-M.
 - Q01 action head: ACT (CVAE) ≈ Mamba-VAE > diffusion policy in this setting (57.4/48.95 room SR) — confidence L.
