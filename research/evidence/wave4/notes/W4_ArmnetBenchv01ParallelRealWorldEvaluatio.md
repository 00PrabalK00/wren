# W4_ArmnetBenchv01ParallelRealWorldEvaluatio — ArmnetBench v0.1: Parallel Real-World Evaluation of Manipulation Policies on a Low-Cost Arm Farm (2026, arXiv 2607.24481)
Setup: SO-101 cells (our exact arm), 2 single-arm + 1 bimanual cell; 3 cameras per cell (single-arm: top + front context cams = RPi Cam 3 @1024×576, wrist = Innomaker 1080p USB @1280×720; bimanual: top + 2 wrist), 20 fps; obs = cams + 5 joint + gripper positions; action = target joint + gripper positions (absolute). 12 tasks (8 single-arm: block_stack, cable_clip/unclip, eye_drops_to_basket/shelf, ring_insert, tool_insert/removal; 4 bimanual). 50 teleop demos per task. 7 policies (ACT, Diffusion Policy, SmolVLA, π0, π0.5, GR00T N1.7, MolmoAct 2), each trained/fine-tuned per task on the same 50 demos. 30 rollouts per task–policy (2,518 total). Cardboard backdrop, ambient office light (uncalibrated). Training recipes/hyperparameters NOT reported.
Claim: a low-cost SO-101 fleet enables large-n real-world comparison; under a 50-demo budget π0.5 leads.
Evidence (Table 5, pooled 12 tasks, strict SR, n≈360): π0.5 47.6, π0 35.1, GR00T N1.7 29.4, Diffusion 26.7, ACT 19.2, MolmoAct 2 18.9, SmolVLA 15.0.
 Per task (Table 6, strict %, order ACT/DP/SmolVLA/π0/π0.5/GR00T/Molmo):
 - block_stack 0/0/7/33/43/27/7; ring_insert 50/23/33/17/47/60/13; tool_insert 20/23/7/40/53/17/3; tool_removal 27/63/3/10/13/30/0; cable_clip 0 for all; cable_unclip 60/7/37/47/70/33/23; eye_drops_to_basket (pick-place, closest to our task) 63/43/23/70/67/67/63; eye_drops_to_shelf 0/17/10/76/70/43/47.
 - Bimanual: fold_tea_towel 10/50/17/50/70/13/20; insert_candle 0/7/7/27/30/3/3; open_lamp_door 0/73/30/7/23/13/33; transfer_cube 0/13/7/47/86/47/13.
 - Single-arm pooled: π0.5 45.4%; bimanual π0.5 52.1%, DP second 35.8%, ACT 2.5%.
Ablations: none (benchmark only). Implicit comparison: ACT is competitive single-arm (best/near-best on ring_insert 50, cable_unclip 60, eye_drops_to_basket 63) but collapses on bimanual (2.5%); DP strongest on tool_removal (63) and open_lamp_door (73).
Failure/limitations: authors: training recipes unreported/untuned ("does not estimate capability ceiling"), initial states unrecorded, no rollout time limit, object wear, front camera misaligned on cell-3 for every policy except MolmoAct 2 and bimanual right-wrist blurry (except MolmoAct 2) — i.e. real camera shift degraded most runs; each task on one cell only. Critical: 30 rollouts/pair → ±~9% SE per cell; per-task rankings noisy; no latency/inference-hardware reporting; SmolVLA's poor showing may reflect integration (async/chunking) recipe.
Conflicts: SmolVLA < ACT on SO-101 with 50 demos contradicts SmolVLA paper's claims of beating ACT on SO-100 tasks — likely recipe/tuning differences and SmolVLA's pretraining data being SO-100 community data (should favour it). π0.5 >> SmolVLA agrees with χ0 finding that π0/π0.5 fine-tune better than other VLAs. ACT strong on simple single-arm pick-place is consistent with ACT paper.
Relevance: The most directly comparable evidence we have: same arm, same 50-demo budget, same cam layout (context + wrist), pick-place task (eye_drops_to_basket) ≈ our pumpkin-to-tray. There, ACT 63%, π0 70%, π0.5 67%, GR00T 67%, Molmo 63%, DP 43%, SmolVLA 23% — for simple pick-place, a small ACT matches 3B VLAs; SmolVLA is worst. π0/π0.5 don't fit 8 GB inference comfortably anyway. Big VLA advantage appears on multi-step/precision tasks (eye_drops_to_shelf: ACT 0 vs π0 76).
Decision impact:
 - Q12 model size: supports small specialist (ACT-size) for simple pick-place at 50 demos; large VLAs (π0/π0.5) help on harder tasks; SmolVLA weakest overall — confidence M (n=30/pair, recipes unreported).
 - Q01 action head: DP (diffusion) vs ACT (CVAE/L1): mixed, task dependent (DP 26.7 vs ACT 19.2 pooled; ACT > DP on pick-place 63 vs 43) — confidence L.
 - Q13 data: 50 demos is enough for ~60–70% on simple pick-place on SO-101 with the best methods, not for contact-rich insertion (cable_clip 0 all) — confidence M.
 - Q02 action space: absolute target joint positions used for all 7 policies on SO-101 (not ablated) — confidence L.
 - Q14 robustness: camera misalignment/blur incidents coincide with depressed rates (uncontrolled) — confidence L.
