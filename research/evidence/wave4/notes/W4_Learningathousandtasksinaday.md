# W4_Learningathousandtasksinaday — Learning a Thousand Tasks in a Day (MT3) (2025, arXiv 2511.10110, Science Robotics)
Setup: Sawyer + Robotiq 2F-85, single head-mounted RealSense D415 RGB-D; inputs = segmented target-object point cloud (Grounding DINO + XMem) + language. Demos 30 Hz, only interaction phase recorded (alignment synthesized). Controlled experiments: (i) 4 micro-skills, 12 seen + 8 unseen tasks, 1→50 demos/task, 3 evals/task (n=36 seen, 24 unseen per point); (ii) fixed 150 demos over 10/30/50 tasks. Positions randomized in 80x45 cm, yaw ±180°. BC backbone MT-ACT+ (PointNet++ + CLIP FiLM, CVAE transformer, action history input, no proprio, terminal action). Large-scale: 1000 tasks from 1 demo each (17 h collection), 2200 rollouts with 5-20 distractors, varied lighting, ±45°.
Claim: in the <10 demos/task regime, decomposing into alignment (pose estimation + motion planning) + interaction (open-loop replay of retrieved demo) gives ~10x data efficiency over monolithic BC; monolithic BC scales better with more data/diversity.
Evidence (mostly figure-only):
 - MT3 with 3 demos/task outperformed all other methods even at 50 demos/task (seen & unseen) — figure only.
 - Decomposition-based methods > monolithic MT-ACT+ across all regimes, gap largest at fewest demos; monolithic accelerates 10–50 demos and narrows the gap (figure, two-proportion z-tests).
 - Diversity: MT-ACT+ with 150 demos over 50 tasks / 10 skills ≈ performance of 600 demos over 12 tasks / 4 skills (diversity trades for data). Retrieval unseen-task performance degrades with more instances (pose vs geometry retrieval trade-off).
 - 1000 tasks: MT3 78.25% seen, 68% unseen (2 trials each). Failure causes (seen): retrieval 22.3%, pose estimation 23.9%, segmentation 19.5%, remaining 29% mostly grasped-object pose inconsistency (20.2%). High-tolerance tasks (wipe, place, grasp) >80%; mm-precision insertion and deformables poor (open-loop).
Ablations: design notes, no numbers: removing proprioception improved spatial generalization (preliminary); target-object point clouds in END-EFFECTOR frame improve BC spatial generalization; interaction-policy augmentation by perturbing EE pose (≤0.9 cm, 5°) and relabeling actions; point-cloud cluster masking (4 of 10 clusters) + noise for occlusion robustness; segmentation for background/distractor robustness.
Failure/limitations: open-loop replay can't react; needs consistent grasp poses; single-interaction tasks; very few trials per task (2-3); no distractors in trajectory path. Different paradigm from end-to-end visuomotor BC.
Conflicts: The "remove proprio" finding agrees with DreamGen's SO-100 observation and copycat literature; contrasts with most VLA/ACT recipes that feed proprio. Monolithic-BC-scales-better echoes standard BC scaling claims; decomposition advantage in tiny data echoes "structure beats capacity" (HumanEgo, OBEYED).
Relevance: Medium. For pumpkin→tray with 50-100 demos we're at the crossover where monolithic BC starts catching up, but the robustness mechanisms (segmented object point cloud from RealSense, EE-frame observation, drop proprio, pose-perturbation relabeling) are directly usable. A decomposed fallback (depth-based pumpkin pose → planned approach, learned grasp/place) is a credible robustness baseline to camera/lighting shift.
Decision impact:
 - Q13 data: <10 demos → structured decomposition ≫ monolithic BC; monolithic benefits from task/instance diversity (150 diverse ≈ 600 narrow) — confidence M (figure, small n per point).
 - Q07 history/proprio: removing proprio improved spatial generalization of BC (preliminary) — confidence L.
 - Q04 3D: segmented object point clouds (EE frame) from single RGB-D enable 1-demo tasks and robustness to distractors/lighting — confidence M.
 - Q05 augmentation: EE-pose perturbation with action relabeling (≤0.9 cm/5°) + point-cloud cluster masking used for robustness — confidence L (no ablation numbers).
 - Q14 robustness: segmentation-based inputs held 78%/68% under 5-20 distractors and varied lighting — confidence M.
