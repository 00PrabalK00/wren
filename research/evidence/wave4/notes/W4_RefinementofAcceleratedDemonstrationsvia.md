# W4_RefinementofAcceleratedDemonstrationsvia — Refinement of Accelerated Demonstrations via Incremental Iterative Reference Learning Control for Fast Contact-Rich Imitation Learning (2026, arXiv 2604.16850)
Setup: UR5e + F/T sensor under compliance control (FDCC), GELLO-style Dynamixel haptic leader, one fixed RealSense D435; tasks: arc erasing (flat/curved), peg-in-hole (2 mm tolerance). Demos time-downsampled (3–10x) and refined by iterative reference learning (IRLC / incremental-speed I2RLC, 3 iterations per speed, one run per condition). ACT (chunk 50, 50 Hz, temporal ensembling) trained on 60 (erasing: 6 demos x 10 noisy replays) or 30 (peg: 1 demo x 30 noisy replays) episodes; 20 rollouts per start position.
Claim: iteratively refining sped-up replays lets IL learn policies up to 6–10x faster than human demos in contact-rich tasks.
Evidence: DTW error at 10x, flat erasing: playback 5.545, IRLC 0.615, I2RLC 0.419; peg 0.634 / 0.156 / 0.085. ACT on I2RLC data: peg-in-hole 100% at seen and ±3 cm unseen positions (IRLC data also 100%), RMS force I2RLC 6.08–7.15 N vs IRLC 6.34–7.46 N; erasing: 53–85% erased area per pattern (~65% avg), below direct playback; authors suspect temporal ensembling latency damps rapid corrections.
Relevance / why low: Contact-rich speed-up on a compliance-controlled industrial arm with F/T sensing; not about our decisions for SO-101 pick-and-place except two weak points: (a) cheap dataset expansion by replaying demos with Gaussian noise on the real robot (1 demo -> 30 episodes sufficed for a fixed-geometry insertion with 3 cm shift), (b) temporal ensembling suspected to add lag at 50 Hz.
Decision impact:
 - Q13 data: replaying 1–6 demos with additive noise on the robot gave enough data for ACT to generalize ±3 cm in peg-in-hole — confidence L (1 task, no ablation vs raw demos)
 - Q06 ensembling: authors attribute reduced erasing quality partly to temporal-ensemble lag (no ablation) — confidence L
