# FutureRTC — Real-Time Robot Execution with Anticipatory-Conditioned Action Chunking (Jiang et al., 2026, arXiv 2607.24008)
Setup: plug-and-play adapter; base VLA FROZEN. State Correction Module (MLP) predicts residual between rolled-forward state and true execution-time state; Observation Prediction Module forecasts execution-time VISION LATENT (from the VLA's own encoder) by motion-aware feature transport conditioned on committed actions; policy-consistency loss aligns chunks from predicted context with chunks from true future context. Delay sampled U[1,dmax] in training (RTX 4090, bs 128, 200k it). Sim: Kinetix (H=8, d 0–4, 12 tasks); LIBERO with LeRobot-released π0.5 and SmolVLA-450M checkpoints, K=25, d∈{5,10,15,20}. Real: bimanual AgileX Cobot Magic, π0.5, 30 Hz, 100 demos/task, 3 tasks × 20 trials; native latency ~170 ms (d≈5: 99 ms forward + 40 ms LAN + 30 ms I/O), plus injected +150 ms (d=10).
Evidence (LIBERO, Table 1, SR % / execution steps):
 - π0.5 d=5/10/15/20: Naive 89.5/82.7/76.3/68.3; TE 87.8/84.3/80.6/74.8; BID 90.3/84.0/76.1/71.3; RTC 89.9/83.2/77.5/73.7; T-RTC 89.4/84.0/77.9/74.0; VLASH 88.0/83.4/79.5/74.5; REMAC 90.8/85.4/78.5/73.9; FutureRTC 94.2/92.4/91.0/88.5.
 - SmolVLA d=5/10/15/20: Naive 70.3/64.5/61.3/56.2; TE 68.4/66.7/62.6/59.7; BID 70.0/65.0/61.7/57.1; RTC 69.7/64.9/62.6/58.7; T-RTC 69.9/65.6/61.7/59.4; VLASH 69.3/65.8/64.7/61.9; REMAC 70.9/66.5/62.2/59.7; FutureRTC 75.8/73.3/71.6/69.4.
 - Kinetix: only 3.0% SR drop from d=0 to d=4; slightly behind training-time methods at d∈{0,1}.
 - Adapter cost: 5.19M params, 3.04 ms.
 - Real (Fig. 6, figure only): highest SR and fewest steps on all 3 tasks at both 170 and 320 ms; naive async degrades sharply; RTC and VLASH "partial relief". Fig. 1 shows RTC and VLASH with jerkier kinematics on Hang Cups (qualitative).
Ablations (Table 2, SmolVLA LIBERO; numbers garbled in extraction): stale baseline collapses with d; +SCM helps at low delay only; +OPM gives the big gain at high delay; +policy-consistency loss adds further.
Failure/limitations: needs training an adapter against demonstrations with future frames; tested with VLA-scale bases; real results figure only, 20 trials. Critical read: in this paper's own reproduction, all baselines (TE, BID, RTC, T-RTC, VLASH) sit within ~2–6 pts of naive async at every delay on LIBERO — contrasts with the big gaps reported in the original papers and in AsyncInferenceStudy (VLASH ~55% flat to d=20 with oracle states). Baseline reproductions by competitors; K=25 with d=20 leaves 5-step overlap.
Conflicts: TE is not worse than RTC here (LIBERO π0.5 d=20: 74.8 vs 73.7), unlike RTC paper's real-robot finding; argues stale IMAGES matter, not only state (VLASH's premise). On quasi-static LIBERO the stale-observation effect should be small, yet they claim large gains — treat with caution.
Relevance: moderate. For us the conclusion "state-only forwarding is insufficient at large delay" matters only if we stay at large delays (~2 s). Better route: cut latency so d is small; then VLASH-level fixes suffice. Adapter concept (5M params, 3 ms) is cheap if needed.
Decision impact:
 - Q10 async: at large delays (d≥10) predicting future visual latents adds robustness beyond state-forwarding — L/M (one paper, competitor-reproduced baselines).
 - Q10 async: all smoothing/continuity baselines give only small gains at d≥10 on LIBERO — supports "reduce d first" — M.
