# AsyncInferenceStudy — Understanding Asynchronous Inference Methods for Vision-Language-Action Models (Agouzoul, ETH/Polytechnique, May 2026, arXiv 2605.08168; code github.com/TheAyos/async-vla-inference)
Setup: the only CONTROLLED head-to-head of IT-RTC, TT-RTC, VLASH, A2C2 (+naive async) in unified codebases. Kinetix: 10 dynamic envs, MLP-Mixer ~310K params, 5 Euler steps, H∈{16,30}, 2048 rollouts per (d,s,method,env), delays up to d=15. LIBERO with SmolVLA (from smolvla_libero; only action expert trained, VLM frozen), H=50, s=50, d∈{0,1,2,4,8,15,20}, 10 rollouts/task (400 per point). A2C2 head: 6-layer transformer + frozen ResNet-18, 32.0M params (20.8M trainable). Sim only. Bachelor-thesis-scale work.
Evidence:
 - Kinetix H=16 (figures): A2C2 highest across nearly all delays, >90% solve at d=8; naive <40% at d=8. TT-RTC (dmax=8) ≈ IT-RTC at d=0–1, better above. VLASH > RTC methods at low delay but < A2C2. Execution-horizon sweep at d=1: all but naive lose only 5–15%.
 - Kinetix H=30: IT-RTC barely beats naive at high delay (linear VJP approximation breaks as prefix/postfix ratio grows). VLASH dmax=15 helps at d≥6 but costs ~10 pts at low delay vs dmax=4. TT-RTC insensitive to dmax (4/8/15 overlap), degrades gracefully OOD.
 - LIBERO SmolVLA (Fig. 4, approximate from text): d=0 IT-RTC ~75%, A2C2 ~70%, TT-RTC/VLASH ~57–61%, naive ~61%. d=20: naive ~10–12%, IT-RTC ~20%, TT-RTC ~25% (dmax 8) / ~33% (dmax 4), VLASH dmax 8/16 ~55–56% (flat), A2C2 ~58%; A2C2 leads by ~10 pts for d∈{4,8,15}.
 - Latency (Table 2, SmolVLA LIBERO chunk, RTX 3090, bs 1): naive 405.2 ms, TT-RTC 402.7, IT-RTC 469.7 (+15.9%), A2C2 serial 412.4 (residual head alone 7.27 ms).
 - FLOPs: SmolVLA vision prefill = 494.6 GFLOPs = 89.8% of chunk cost; IT-RTC 3× denoise-loop FLOPs → 1.20× total; A2C2 with s=50 residual calls → 1.75× naive per chunk.
Failure/limitations: VLASH on LIBERO used ORACLE future states (state/action dims differ), so its LIBERO stability is an upper bound; fixed integer delays (real delay jitters); hyperparameters not retuned; no real robot; no smoothness metric; 10 rollouts/task.
Conflicts: ranking flips between benchmarks — TT-RTC best trade-off on short-chunk Kinetix but degrades with long (H=50) SmolVLA chunks; A2C2 is the most chunk-size-tolerant. IT-RTC best at d=0 on LIBERO. Contradicts VLASH's own claim of dominance over RTC at low delay on LIBERO-like settings? (VLASH paper had no RTC on LIBERO.) Agrees with DEFLECT that inpainting collapses when overlap is small.
Relevance: very high — same model family (SmolVLA) as ours. Key facts for us: (1) SmolVLA chunk ≈ 405 ms even on an RTX 3090 in this LeRobot setup, 90% of it vision prefill → cutting denoising steps alone won't fix our 2 s; (2) with H=50 at our latencies, prefix methods weaken; state-forwarding (VLASH) and per-step residual correction (A2C2) are the robust ones; (3) naive async (what we run) collapses with delay.
Decision impact:
 - Q10 async: never run naive async at d≫1; prefer VLASH (wide dmax) and/or TT-RTC; A2C2 if willing to train a 20M head — M (sim, controlled, 400 eps/point).
 - Q10 latency: SmolVLA cost is dominated by vision/VLM prefill (89.8%) — M/H.
 - Q06 chunking: shorter chunks favour prefix-conditioning methods; long chunks favour residual/state-forward — L/M.
