# SAIL — Faster-than-Demonstration Execution of Imitation Learning Policies (Arachchige et al., Georgia Tech, CoRL 2025, arXiv 2506.11948)
Setup: Diffusion Policy base; sim RoboMimic/MimicGen Lift, Can, Square, Stack, Mug Cleanup; real Franka Panda + bimanual UR5, 7 tasks, 10 rollouts per task per method, SAIL at 5× vs DP-Fast at 5×. Metrics: success, throughput-with-regret (TPR), avg time to success, speedup-over-demo, plus smoothness (consistency, SPARC, LDLJ, WED).
Method (4 parts): (1) Error-Adaptive Guidance: classifier-free guidance conditioning the next chunk on the tail of the previous plan, with guidance switched off when end-effector tracking error exceeds a threshold ρ (conditioning on a plan the robot did not follow is OOD); (2) predict REACHED poses (robot's actual trajectory) instead of COMMANDED teleop poses, and track with a high-gain controller; (3) adaptive speed factor c_t (slow in precision phases); (4) latency-aware action scheduling: keep executing the old plan during sensing+inference delay, discard stale actions on arrival; minimum step interval δ_lb > δ_delay/(H_p − H_c) to avoid action exhaustion.
Evidence:
 - Sim: "up to 3× throughput of DP on Can and Stack without sacrificing success"; up to 4× speedup; TPR keeps rising up to c=0.1 (10×) in ideal sim (Fig. 6).
 - Sim Table 1 (Lift SR): DP 1.00, DP-Fast 0.95, AWE 1.00, Aggregated actions 0.91, BID-Fast 0.86, SAIL 1.00 (other cells garbled in extraction).
 - Real (Table 2): TPR and speedup improved on 6/7 tasks vs DP-Fast; up to 3.2× over demo speed; bimanual serving 5.4× throughput and ~1.8× success; SAIL slightly worse on wiping.
Ablations: using commanded instead of reached poses → 55% SR drop on Square and −0.08 avg TPR; high-gain controllers are very sensitive to noisy references (needs EAG smoothing); removing adaptive speed hurts precision tasks; EAG error cutoff crucial (App. H).
Failure/limitations: requires a high-fidelity, high-gain tracking controller; 10 real rollouts per task; Franka/UR5 not low-cost servos. Critical read: BID-Fast (sampling-based consistency) was worse than DP-Fast in sim — coherence without accounting for tracking error can hurt.
Conflicts: agrees with RTC/Legato/TT-RTC that conditioning on the previous plan gives smooth motion, but adds that the condition must be dropped when the robot did not track it — relevant for backlash-prone hardware. Agrees with VLASH that "action quantization"/speedup is possible post-hoc.
Relevance: moderate-high. (a) SO-101 leader/follower data: action = leader (commanded) and state = follower (reached); SAIL says training on reached poses matters for FAST execution with stiff tracking — for us at demo speed this is secondary, but the backlash gap (commanded ≠ reached) is exactly the tracking error that invalidates prefix conditioning. (b) The action-scheduling rule (execute old plan during delay, drop stale actions by timestamp) is the right async bookkeeping.
Decision impact:
 - Q10 async: timestamp-based scheduling that discards stale actions + continuity guidance gated by tracking error — M (sim + 10-trial real).
 - Q10 speed: faster-than-demo execution feasible (up to 3.2× real) but needs good tracking — L for SO-101.
 - Q02 action space: reached-pose targets beat commanded poses at high speed (Square −55% with commanded) — M (sim).
