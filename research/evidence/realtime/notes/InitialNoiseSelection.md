# InitialNoiseSelection (PAINT) — Start Right, Arrive Right: Asynchronous Execution via Initial Noise Selection (Ho et al., VinRobotics, Jun 2026, arXiv 2606.19774)
Setup: training-free inference method for flow policies. Instead of steering the ODE toward the committed prefix (RTC guidance with VJPs), invert the previous chunk's committed prefix back to noise with backward Euler, use that as the prefix part of the initial noise, run the UNMODIFIED flow ODE, then "repaint" (replace prefix). Sim: 12 Kinetix envs, d 0–4, vs naive, TE, B-spline refit, BID, RTC. Real: GR00T-N1.5 (H=16, N=4 steps) and π0 (H=50, N=10), remote LAN inference, 6 tasks (block stacking, toy in drawer, banana in pot, towel flinging, shorts folding, humanoid part placing), 20 trials per method-task.
Evidence:
 - Sim (figures): naive degrades sharply; TE and B-spline refitting give limited robustness (post-hoc smoothing leaves prefix mismatch ≈ naive); BID partial at higher compute; PAINT-Euler best robustness and lowest prefix mismatch without gradients. PAINT and RTC both benefit from SHORTER execution horizons.
 - Real (Table 1, SR / avg time-to-success s / consistency CON): GR00T-N1.5 Block stacking: TE(sync) 0.55/28.27/–, RTC 0.75/16.04/0.030, PAINT 0.75/15.32/0.023. Toy in drawer: 0.60/23.19, 0.75/17.85/0.025, 0.85/17.08/0.023. Banana in pot: 0.60/29.57, 0.70/30.15/0.031, 0.70/29.79/0.026. π0 Block stacking: TE 0.20/58.35, RTC 0.50/15.30/0.034, PAINT 0.50/14.90; toy in drawer TE 0.15, RTC 0.65, PAINT 0.65.
 - Inversion ablation: backward Euler best quality/complexity vs DPM-Solver-2, single-step reverse flow matching, optimisation, no inversion (figure).
Failure/limitations (authors): relies on noise↔action position locality (OT flow matching encourages it; may break with strong cross-position attention or very multimodal distributions); backward Euler assumes near-straight flows. Critical read: TE baseline was run SYNCHRONOUSLY, so its long completion times reflect pauses, not TE itself; 20 trials/task; PAINT ≈ RTC in SR on most tasks, gains mainly in consistency and no gradient cost.
Conflicts: agrees with RTC that averaging/smoothing (TE, B-splines) doesn't fix prefix mismatch; offers RTC-level quality at lower cost (inversion = extra N forward passes, no backward).
Relevance: moderate: a training-free drop-in for a flow head if we cannot retrain; costs N extra forward passes of the (small) action expert only — the VLM prefix is not rerun. For a few-step head this is cheap.
Decision impact:
 - Q10 async: training-free continuity via noise inversion ≈ RTC success, better consistency, no VJP — M (20 trials, 2 VLAs).
 - Q10: post-hoc smoothing (TE/B-spline) insufficient for boundary consistency — M.
