# StreamingDP — Streaming Diffusion Policy: Fast Policy Synthesis with Variable Noise Diffusion Models (Høeg, Du, Egeland, 2024, arXiv 2406.04806)
Setup: Diffusion Policy U-Net whose diffusion-step encoder accepts a per-element noise level. A rolling action buffer holds a partially denoised trajectory: the next action is clean, later actions increasingly noisy; each new observation applies a few denoising steps to the buffer shifted by one chunk (diffusion-forcing / rolling diffusion idea). Training noise: 80% chunk-wise + 20% constant corruption (ablation on Push-T state). Sim: image-based Push-T and Robomimic Lift/Can/Square/Transport/ToolHang, 10 checkpoints × 3 seeds; baselines DP and Consistency Policy (teacher+student each trained same epochs = 2× training). Real: Push-T, 134 demos, 600 epochs, 20 rollouts, DDIM-16 for DP and SDP, executed SYNCHRONOUSLY for all methods.
Evidence:
 - Sim (Table III, numbers not extracted): SDP comparable to DP incl. Transport; Consistency Policy "invariably equal or less" and worse on intricate tasks despite 2× training.
 - Sampling time falls with longer buffers (Fig. 4); horizon 19 vs 15: 2.4 s → 1.8 s in one reported setting (CPU/overall eval time, not per-chunk).
 - Real Push-T (Fig. 7): success DP 0.50 (0.13 s/chunk), Consistency Policy 0.40 (0.01 s), SDP 0.85 (0.07 s). Failure mode of DP/CP: small oscillatory replanning movements during slow precise pushes; SDP's persistent buffer avoids it.
Ablations: action primers (zero init best), noise schemes: linear or constant alone perform poorly; chunk-wise + constant mix best (Table II, numbers not extracted).
Failure/limitations: requires sequential chunk enactment (cannot overlap next-chunk denoising with execution in their design) — so it reduces latency rather than hiding it; real = one task, 20 trials. Critical read: sim numbers not reproduced here; 1-step Consistency Policy underperforming contradicts ConsistencyPolicy paper's parity claims.
Conflicts: supports the "persistent buffer / warm-start from previous plan" idea shared with πR2 (staircase schedule), Legato and RTC; agrees with BID that re-sampling independent chunks causes oscillation in precise phases.
Relevance: moderate: shows that carrying a partially denoised plan across observations both speeds sampling (~2× vs DDIM-16) and removes replanning jitter — the same mechanism as training-time RTC/πR2. For a small flow head this is an alternative to prefix conditioning, but needs a per-token noise-level architecture.
Decision impact:
 - Q10 smoothness: rolling partially denoised buffer removes replanning oscillation (real 0.85 vs 0.50) — L/M (one real task, 20 trials).
 - Q01 head: per-token noise level conditioning is the enabling architectural change (shared with TT-RTC/πR2) — M.
