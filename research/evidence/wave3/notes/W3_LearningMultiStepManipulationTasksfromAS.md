# W3_LearningMultiStepManipulationTasksfromAS — Learning Multi-Step Manipulation Tasks from A Single Human Demonstration (2024, arXiv id not in text; CMU, Dingkun Guo)
Setup: Modular (non-learned policy) pipeline: one RGB-D human video -> Grounded-SAM segmentation + ICP object poses -> segmented into robot primitives -> motion planning on Franka FR3; dishwashing (faucet on/off, pick, rinse, place), 5 trials per condition.
Claim/Evidence: per-step 50-100% success, whole 5-step task 20-40%; in lab with seen positions >=80% per step but full task 20-40% (error compounding); unseen locations 40-80% per step, full 20%; unseen home kitchen faucet 50%. Failures: pose estimation 50%, motion planning 26%.
Relevance/Decision impact: No evidence bearing on our learned-policy design decisions (no BC, no ablations, 5 trials); only a generic reminder that per-step errors compound across multi-step tasks and that off-the-shelf open-vocabulary segmentation (Grounded-SAM) gives object/instance generalization for modular systems. No ledger entry beyond a low-confidence Q14 note.
 - Q14 robustness: open-vocabulary segmentation+pose pipeline transfers to new bowls/cups/kitchen but pose estimation is the dominant failure (50%) — confidence L.
