# W3_CLIPortWhatandWherePathwaysforRoboticMan — CLIPort: What and Where Pathways for Robotic Manipulation (2021, CoRL, arXiv 2109.12098)
Setup: Ravens/PyBullet UR5e suction, 10 language-conditioned tabletop tasks (seen/unseen colors/objects), input = top-down RGB-D orthographic reconstruction from 3 noiseless 640x480 cams; action = one pick pose + one place pose (Transporter affordance maps), NOT continuous control. Trained with 1/10/100/1000 demos, 100 eval instances. Two streams: semantic (frozen-init CLIP ResNet50 + CLIP sentence encoder, fine-tuned decoder with language fusion) and spatial (Transporter FCN on RGB-D). Real: Franka Panda, 9 tasks, one multi-task model, 5–10 demos per task (179 image-action samples total), 5–10 test runs per task.
Claim: fusing a pretrained CLIP semantic stream with a spatial RGB-D Transporter stream gives data-efficient language-conditioned pick-and-place and multi-task transfer.
Evidence:
 - Averages (Fig 3): Transporter-only (no language) saturates ~50%, CLIP-only (no depth/spatial stream) ~76%, CLIPort single >90%; ~86% on most tasks with 100 demos.
 - Multi-task beats single-task in 41/72 = 57% of evaluations (same demos per task), mostly at <=100 demos; worse on long-horizon align-rope.
 - Transfer of attributes from other tasks: put-blocks-in-bowls-unseen-colors (n=1000) 45.8 → 75.7.
 - Real (Table 2): stack blocks 70%, put blocks in bowl 65%, pack objects 60%, move rook 70%, fold cloth 57%, read text 55%, rope loop 60%, sweep beans 60.6%, pick cherries 75% from 2–10 demos per task.
Ablations (App. F, Table 5, stack-block-pyramid-seq seen, n = 1/10/100/1000): CLIPort 28.3/64.7/93.3/98.8; ImageNet-RN50 + DistilBERT two-stream 5.3/35.0/89.0/97.5; untrained semantic stream 3.0/12.7/61.5/51.2. Unseen colors: 13.7/24.3/31.2/41.3 vs RN50-BERT 6.2/12.2/21.5/30.7. Injecting language into the spatial (Transporter) stream directly ("one-stream language transporter") performs very poorly — high-level language features corrupt low-level spatial features. Removing encoder→decoder skip connections collapses performance.
Failure/limitations: authors: two-step pick/place primitive only, no dexterous/6-DoF continuous control, no task-completion prediction; policy exploited dataset bias in real put-blocks-in-bowl (one yellow→blue example made it ignore language) — "at least 50–100 demos" estimated necessary for robust real performance. Critical read: noiseless sim cameras, top-down orthographic view needs calibrated multi-cam fusion; real eval tiny (5–10 runs).
Conflicts: consistent with later findings that pretrained vision-language features give largest gains in the few-demo regime and converge with ImageNet features at 1000 demos; the spatial-precision gap of CLIP-only matches reports that CLIP/SigLIP features are coarse spatially (DINO-type or depth streams add precision).
Relevance: moderate for Q03/Q08, low for architecture (primitive-based). For SO-101: pretrained VL encoder matters most at our 50–100-demo scale; keep language fusion out of the low-level spatial pathway (e.g. FiLM on later layers / separate stream); ensure language-balanced data when adding a second object to avoid bias-exploitation.
Decision impact:
 - Q03 vision encoder: CLIP-pretrained > ImageNet > untrained at few demos (10 demos: 64.7 vs 35.0 vs 12.7), converging at 1000 — confidence M (sim, primitive actions).
 - Q08 language: multi-task language conditioning helps in 57% of cases at equal per-task data; language injected into spatial stream hurts; real model exploits bias with unbalanced language data — confidence M.
 - Q04 depth: RGB-D spatial stream needed for precision (CLIP-only RGB saturates ~76% vs >90%) — confidence L-M (sim, top-down fused cams).
 - Q13 data: authors estimate >=50–100 demos needed for robust real performance; balanced coverage of language/attribute combos matters — confidence L.
