# W3_CRAFTVideoDiffusionforBimanualRobotDataG — CRAFT: Video Diffusion for Bimanual Robot Data Generation (2026, arXiv 2604.03552)
Setup: Real bimanual Franka FR3 (GELLO teleop), 1 or 3 RealSense D435i (third-person + 2 wrists), ACT policy trained on RTX 5090; 3 real tasks (Lift Roller, Place Cans in Plasticbox, Stack Bowls), 50–100 real demos per task, 20 trials per condition. Sim: RoboTwin (UR5→Franka cross-embodiment), 3 seeds. Pipeline: digital-twin replay of trajectories with new object poses in sim → Canny edges → zero-shot Wan2.1-Fun-Control 1.3B video diffusion conditioned on a real reference image (edited for lighting/background/color/viewpoint) → photoreal videos with inherited action labels; multi-view via tiling. 1000 generated demos per task.
Claim: Edge-conditioned video diffusion generates action-consistent, temporally coherent, visually diverse demos that make ACT robust to lighting, background, object color, camera view, and embodiment changes, beating 2D augmentation baselines.
Evidence (Table III, real, successes /20; order LR / PC / SB; each test varies only that factor):
 - Unseen lighting (blue/green/red/yellow ambient): ACT no aug 3/1/0; CRAFT pose-only 5/3/2; Color Jitter 13/9/8; CRAFT 17/14/12.
 - Unseen background: no aug 4/0/0; pose-only 7/2/3; RoboEngine (segment+inpaint) 4/5/6; CRAFT 18/15/10.
 - Camera view (4 new perspectives): no aug 6/3/2; pose-only 13/5/7; VISTA NVS 14/8/6; CRAFT 19/18/18.
 - Object color (train red, test gray): no aug 2/0/1; pose-only 5/2/3; SAM3 seg+color jitter 15/9/11; CRAFT 18/18/17.
 - Wrist+3rd person (3 views): real-only 15/11/13; pose-only 13/8/10; CRAFT 20/19/20.
 - Cross-embodiment real (xArm7→Franka, /20): collected target 5/2/3; Shadow 2/1/1; CRAFT(target) 4/1/2; CRAFT 17/15/16. Sim (UR5→Franka): target 55/69/59%; CRAFT 82.6/89.3/86.0%.
Ablations: Canny-edge conditioning vs raw sim frames as control (Stack Bowls sim, no aug): 21.6% vs 10.3% (target demos 59.0%).
Failure/limitations: CRAFT uses 1000 generated + real demos while baselines use only 50–100 real → count and diversity confounded; test conditions are single-factor; requires digital twin (AprilTags, object meshes) and heavy video generation GPUs (RTX 5090 / 3x RTX 6000); tasks are simple; ACT only. Color jitter baseline is a crude per-channel scale — still gave 3→13 on LR.
Conflicts: Agrees with RoboSplat/RoboEngine-type works that visual augmentation is the dominant lever for small-policy robustness; shows cheap Color Jitter already recovers a big part of lighting robustness (e.g. 3→13, 1→9, 0→8), consistent with GreenAug/DABI results; generative augmentation adds a further 3–5/20. Segmentation-based augmentations (RoboEngine, SAM3) are hurt by segmentation errors at gripper–object contact.
Relevance: Highly relevant to our lighting / different-pumpkin / camera-shift failures with an ACT-class policy at 50–100 demos. Practical takeaways: (1) color/lighting jitter is a must and cheap; (2) object-color/appearance shift needs object-level recolor (SAM-based works partially: 2→15); (3) camera-shift robustness needs viewpoint-diverse data — pose-only diversity already helps (6→13); (4) full video-diffusion pipeline likely too heavy for an 8 GB laptop but could be run offline elsewhere.
Decision impact:
 - Q05 augmentation: real ACT: color jitter lifts unseen-lighting success 1–3/20 → 8–13/20; generative relighting 12–17/20; object recolor via SAM 0–2 → 9–15/20; generative 17–18/20 — strongly supports photometric aug as baseline, generative aug as upgrade — confidence H (real, per-factor tests, though 20 trials and count-confounded).
 - Q14 robustness: ACT trained on 50–100 clean demos collapses to 0–6/20 under unseen lighting, background, color, or camera view — confidence H.
 - Q11 cameras: novel camera views: no aug 2–6/20 vs view-augmented 18–19/20; also wrist+3rd person policy real-only 11–15/20 — supports multi-view/viewpoint augmentation for camera-shift robustness — confidence M.
 - Q13 data: object-pose diversity alone (pose-only, 2x demos) improves out-of-factor robustness modestly (e.g. camera 6→13 LR) — confidence L.
