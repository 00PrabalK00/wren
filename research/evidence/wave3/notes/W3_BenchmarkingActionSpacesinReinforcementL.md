# W3_BenchmarkingActionSpacesinReinforcementL — Benchmarking Action Spaces in Reinforcement Learning for Vision-based Robotic Manipulation (2026, arXiv id not in text)
Setup: Franka Panda, wrist RealSense D405, 64x64 RGB + proprio + previous action; PPO in MuJoCo with domain randomization (camera pose, lighting, contrast/saturation, textures, stiffness/damping), zero-shot sim-to-real; 2 tasks (pick cuboid, push cuboid); 25 Hz (40 ms cycle); small 3-layer CNN actor-critic; 10 seeds x 1024 envs; real eval 12 (pick) / 10 (push) trials per action space. Action spaces: pose increment dx, pose velocity xdot (both via analytic IK), joint position increment dq, joint velocity v.
Claim: action space strongly affects sim-to-real; joint velocity best for smoothness + task performance; Cartesian (IK-based) spaces transfer worst.
Evidence (Table III, real): Pick success dx 41.67, xdot 0.00, dq 100, v 100; pick time dq 2.12 s, v 3.58 s; jerk (m/s^3) dx 26.88, xdot 24.55, dq 52.59, v 16.03 (scripted 11.43). Push median distance dx 0.050, xdot 0.000, dq 0.104, v 0.405 m; jerk dx 60.27, xdot 24.85, dq 24.37, v 14.25; collisions v 8.0 (forceful contacts), dq 0.
 - In sim all action spaces reach 0.98-1.00 pick success; differences emerge only on the real robot.
Ablations: the action-space comparison itself; authors explain velocity actuation low-pass-filters policy noise (integration), whereas position targets are amplified by Kp -> higher jerk (dq jerk 52.6 vs v 16.0 for pick).
Failure/limitations: RL + sim-to-real, not imitation learning; very few real trials (10-12), single object; position control needed careful impedance tuning; Franka torque-controlled robot — velocity interface semantics differ from Feetech position servos. Only incremental/velocity spaces tested (no absolute positions).
Conflicts: ACT and many IL papers favor absolute joint position targets; this study (RL, no absolute option) favors joint velocity. Agreement across both: joint space > Cartesian/IK space on real hardware (IK amplifies model errors, singularities). Different regime: RL-policy output noise is high-frequency; chunked IL output is smoother so the jerk argument is weaker.
Relevance: SO-101 Feetech servos accept position targets only; joint velocity would have to be integrated on the host — the low-pass argument then applies in software. The strongest usable signal: stay in joint space (SO-101 has 5-DoF arm, IK is poor), and if jerk is the problem, smoothing/integrating commands helps. 
Decision impact:
 - Q02 action space: supports joint space over Cartesian/IK-based (real pick 100 vs 41.7/0) — M (RL sim-to-real, 12 trials, but large effect)
 - Q02 action space: joint velocity smoother than joint position increments (jerk 16 vs 53) — L (RL, torque robot; not IL chunks)
 - Q10 smoothness: command integration/low-pass filtering reduces jerk — L
