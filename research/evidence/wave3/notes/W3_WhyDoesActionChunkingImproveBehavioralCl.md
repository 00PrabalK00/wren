# W3_WhyDoesActionChunkingImproveBehavioralCl — Why Does Action Chunking Improve Behavioral Cloning Performance in Robotic Control? (2026, arXiv 2608.02547)
Setup: diffusion policies (image obs) on LIBERO (90 + 10/Spatial/Goal/Object suites, 50 human demos/task, >=3 seeds) and Robomimic PH (Can, Square, Transport, Tool Hang); real Franka, 3 pick-place tasks (carrot->bowl, bread from toaster, sushi->cup), 50 human demos each, delta joint position control at 15 Hz, chunk k=20, 50 rollouts per bar; sim demos at 20 Hz. Inference interleaved with execution in real (adds 1-step delay to all methods).
Claim: chunking's benefit is NOT temporal consistency, horizon reduction or representation learning; it comes from (1) predicting from a past observation (captures non-Markovian human pauses at "decision boundaries" and provably reduces compounding error from exponential to ~(k+1)^(H/k)), and (2) an implicit ensemble over learned temporal relationships a_t|o_{t-i}. A chunk policy deployed as a randomized-delay ensemble (RDE) matches chunking; explicit ensembles beat it.
Evidence (Table 1, success %, Markovian / AC(n) / Delay(n) / AC-RDE / AC-TE uniform temporal ensemble):
 - LIBERO-90: 68.9 / 89.2 / 94.0 / 93.6 / 92.7. LIBERO-10: 19.8 / 88.7 / 86.8 / 88.5 / 86.0. Spatial 58.1 / 91.6 / 92.0 / 92.7 / 91.1. Goal 62.7 / 96.5 / 96.3 / 96.3 / 96.7. Object 59.8 / 98.0 / 97.4 / 97.3 / 97.6.
 - Robomimic Can 83.7 / 97.2 / 93.5 / 96.7 / 96.2; Square 69.0 / 85.4 / 80.8 / 82.4 / 80.6; Transport 3.3 / 12.6 / 7.9 / 12.1 / 12.2; Tool Hang 28.0 / 75.2 / 51.6 / 71.8 / 42.2.
 - Explicit ensembles (Table 2, AC-Ens / Delay-Ens / RDE-Ens): LIBERO-90 94.1 / 95.0 / 94.6; LIBERO-10 90.6 / 90.2 / 89.5; Can 98.5 / 97.7 / 98.7; Square 88.3 / 87.7 / 85.7; Transport 41.5 / 38.9 / 38.4 (vs AC 12.6, ~+29 pts); Tool Hang 87.6 / 84.9 / 86.4 (vs 75.2).
 - Real (Fig 10, figure only, qualitative): Markovian poor; chunking much better; single delayed policy captures much but not all; RDE matches or exceeds chunking on all 3 tasks.
Ablations: delay sweep — a_t best predicted from o_{t-10} on LIBERO (validation MSE lowest for delays ~5-15); non-Markovianity (val-loss gap) poorly correlates with per-task chunking gain; training on chunks but executing 1 step helps vs Markovian but equals a directly trained delay-5 policy (representation benefit only for short horizons); uniform temporal ensembling (AC-TE) is worse than random-delay sampling (Tool Hang 42.2 vs 71.8). Authors note full history-conditioned policies "typically very poor" (sample complexity) vs delayed policies. At 50-60 Hz control delayed policies FAIL to match chunking; treating 5-step sub-chunks as actions (effective 10-20 Hz) restores performance — temporal consistency matters only at high control rates.
Failure/limitations: explicit ensembles multiply compute (m models); real results figure-only, 3 easy pick-place tasks; theory assumes smooth deterministic dynamics; real latency effects acknowledged as unmeasured confound ("inference pauses").
Conflicts: challenges ACT's temporal-consistency rationale and the common belief that chunking helps by horizon reduction; agrees with ACT that ensembling helps but finds uniform averaging inferior to randomized delay sampling (authors note ACT-style exponential weighting mutes the ensembling effect); FurnitureVLA found recency-weighted TE best and uniform TE worst — both agree uniform averaging is not ideal, and both show ensembles across time help. Footnote on poor history conditioning agrees with copycat/causal-confusion literature (Q07).
Relevance: high for our jerky chunk-boundary problem and 10 vs 30 fps question: (1) at 10-20 Hz, chunking's value is conditioning on past obs + ensembling, so a per-step randomized-delay readout of the last k predicted chunks (RDE) gives smooth-ish per-step control without hard chunk boundaries and without extra model calls if chunks are cached; (2) if we go to 30 fps, keep chunking (temporal consistency matters at high rates) or subsample actions; (3) a small ensemble (2-3 small DP/ACT heads) could be affordable on 8 GB and gave large gains on hard tasks; (4) avoid long observation history.
Decision impact:
 - Q06 chunking: chunking >> single-step (LIBERO-10 19.8 -> 88.7; Tool Hang 28.0 -> 75.2); benefit = delayed-obs prediction + implicit ensemble; RDE deployment matches AC — H for sim (many tasks, seeds), M for real.
 - Q06/Q10 temporal ensembling: uniform averaging can hurt (Tool Hang 42.2 vs AC 75.2); randomized-delay sampling or explicit ensembles better — M.
 - Q10 smoothness/latency: interleaving inference with execution (1-step delay) works; RDE offers per-step updates from cached chunks — L/M (real figure only).
 - Q07 history: full history conditioning "typically very poor"; delayed single-frame conditioning captures needed non-Markovianity — M (authors' observation, no table).
 - Q01/Q12: explicit ensemble of m diffusion policies +2 to +29 pts over single AC — M (sim).
 - Q02 frequency: at 50-60 Hz chunk temporal consistency matters; ~10-20 Hz effective rate is the sweet spot for human demos — L/M (qualitative).
