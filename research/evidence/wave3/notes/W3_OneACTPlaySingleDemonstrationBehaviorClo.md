# W3_OneACTPlaySingleDemonstrationBehaviorClo — One ACT Play: Single Demonstration Behavior Cloning with Action Chunking Transformers (2023, arXiv 2309.10175)
Setup: Panda-Gym (PyBullet) Franka, 3 block tasks (push-as-planar-pick-place, pick&place, stack), gripper orientation fixed (4-DoF EE position+gripper). ONE VR demonstration, augmented by rotation/translation/uniform-scale transforms of the trajectory, replayed with P-controller, failures discarded -> 25-400 synthetic demos. Policy = ACT (CVAE, chunking, temporal ensembling), EE position actions, 2 cams in sim (wrist + static), 3 cams on hardware. Real: Franka + RealSense D415 (RGB only), push task only. Trials per setting not clearly stated (sim results reported with std).
Claim: a single demo + linear-transform augmentation + ACT can learn block tasks; a std-adaptive temporal ensembling (dynamic k and switching to pure chunk replay when predictions disagree) beats ACT's fixed exponential ensembling.
Evidence: sim success with 400 augmented demos: push ~98-99%, pick&place ~99%, stack 78.4% (ours) vs 60.8% (ACT baseline ensembling). Success increases with #augmented demos (Table I baseline: push 29.2/60.8/78.0/92.0/98.8 for 25/50/100/200/400; pick&place 22.4/42.8/70.0/84.0/98.8). Hardware push: 35% / 70% / 90% with 50 / 100 / 200 augmented demos.
Ablations (Table I, sim):
 - Ensembling: baseline vs "only resetting" (slightly worse than baseline) vs "only dynamic k" (slightly better with β=0.5) vs combined (best, β=1). Stack at 400 demos: baseline 60.8 → combined 78.4 (reported in conclusion). Method sensitive to β.
 - #demos: monotone gains, more demos needed for harder task (stack).
Failure/limitations: augmentation assumes task is linearly transformable between start/goal; requires cheap autonomous replay; blocks only, fixed orientation. Critical read: synthetic demos are near-unimodal replays of one trajectory, so conclusions about ensembling under multimodality are indirect; real eval is one task, small N, unclear trial count.
Conflicts: agrees with ACT that chunking+ensembling helps; shows vanilla exponential ensembling can hurt when earlier chunk predictions become stale after unexpected state changes — consistent with later reports (e.g. RTC/bidirectional decoding) that naive averaging of stale chunks is suboptimal for reactivity/multimodality.
Relevance: Moderate. (1) For our chunk-boundary jerk: a cheap, training-free fix is to weight/ignore stale chunk predictions based on inter-prediction std — directly implementable in LeRobot ACT inference. (2) Trajectory-transform augmentation of a demo (MimicGen-lite) is a possible data-multiplication route for pick-place with varying object/tray positions on SO-101, but requires scripted replay and success checking.
Decision impact:
 - Q06 temporal ensembling: supports disagreement-aware ensembling over fixed exponential TE (stack 60.8→78.4) — L/M (sim, near-unimodal synthetic data)
 - Q10 chunk-boundary smoothness: std-gated switching between ensembling and pure chunk replay is a training-free option — L
 - Q05 synthetic demos: trajectory-transform augmentation from 1 demo reaches 90% on real push with 200 replays — L (single real task)
 - Q13 data: success monotone in #demos (real push 35/70/90% at 50/100/200) — L
