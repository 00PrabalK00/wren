# W3_VisualPolicyLearningThroughMultiCameraVi — Visual-Policy Learning through Multi-Camera View to Single-Camera View Knowledge Distillation for Robot Manipulation Tasks (2023, RA-L, arXiv 2303.07026)
Setup: PyBullet Franka, SAC (RL) with 50 3D-mouse human demos seeded in replay; 84x84 RGB, 12-layer conv encoder + self-attention; action EE xyz + yaw + gripper. Teachers: 1 cam (front), 2 cams (front + wrist/egocentric), 3 cams (front + side + wrist), fixed viewpoints. Students: single randomized front camera (position, FOV, aspect ratio; curriculum widening range), trained DAgger-style on teacher actions + feature MSE; pixel-shift + colour jitter aug everywhere. Eval 100 fixed / 100 random-view trials x 3 seeds. Real: zero-shot sim-to-real on Panda with a RealSense D435i, cube and mug lift, 4 random camera views x 20 trials.
Claim: distilling a multi-view (incl. wrist) teacher into a single-camera student trained with heavy camera randomization yields camera-pose-robust single-view policies; camera randomization alone fails.
Evidence (success %, fixed front view / random front view):
 - Cube: teacher 1cam 96.5 / 2.0; teacher 2cam (front+wrist) 98.6 / 23.3; cross-view-attention 2cam baseline 99.3 / 39.6; teacher 3cam 99.3 / 68.7; student from 1cam 93.0 / 84.6; student from 2cam 96.6 / 88.7; student from 3cam 99.0 / 96.0.
 - Mug (asymmetric): teacher 1cam 0 / 0 (cannot learn); 2cam 99.3 / 30.6; cross-view 99.3 / 37.3; 3cam 100 / 41.3; student 2cam 89.6 / 77.0; student 3cam 91.6 / 81.7.
 - Camera randomization WITHOUT distillation: 0% success on both tasks (stated).
 - Real zero-shot, 4 random views x 20 trials: cube student-1cam 40/60/20/35 vs student-3cam 80/80/60/50; mug student-3cam 70/70/65/50, + joint-state input 80/80/75/65.
Ablations: feature alignment by Euclidean MSE > pairwise-similarity matching (figure only); adding joint state +10–15 pts real per view; more teacher views -> better students; training time +9.1% (2cam) / +16% (3cam).
Failure/limitations: RL in sim with privileged teacher; single simple lift tasks; low 84x84 resolution; real results are sim-to-real zero-shot with 20 trials/view; wrist camera only in teacher (student has no wrist cam) so the value of a wrist cam at deployment is not tested.
Conflicts: consistent with BeyondViewpoint note (multi-view supervision helps even single-view policies) and with the common finding that a fixed-view policy collapses under camera moves (cube 96.5 -> 2.0). Shows naive camera randomization can make learning fail — contrasts with "just randomize" advice; the teacher signal makes heavy randomization learnable.
Relevance: moderate-high for our "slightly moved camera" failure: evidence that fixed single-view policies are extremely brittle to camera pose, that a wrist camera plus a second scene view makes even the fixed-view teacher far more robust (random-view 2 -> 23–69%), and that viewpoint randomization during training (via multi-view data or synthetic views) is what buys robustness. For BC with 50–100 demos we cannot do DAgger from an RL teacher, but we can emulate: record extra views / synthesize views and add proprio state.
Decision impact:
 - Q11 cameras: supports adding a wrist camera + extra views (teacher random-view cube 2.0 -> 23.3 -> 68.7 with 1/2/3 cams; mug unlearnable with front only) — confidence M (sim RL, but large effects, 3 seeds x 100 trials).
 - Q14 robustness (camera shift): fixed-view policy collapses under camera perturbation (96.5 -> 2.0); viewpoint randomization + multi-view supervision restores 96.0 sim, 50–80% real — confidence M.
 - Q05 augmentation: camera-pose randomization alone (no teacher) -> 0% (RL) — caution that extreme view randomization can prevent learning — confidence L (RL setting, may not transfer to BC).
 - Q07/state input: adding joint state improves real zero-shot by 10–15 pts per view — confidence L.
