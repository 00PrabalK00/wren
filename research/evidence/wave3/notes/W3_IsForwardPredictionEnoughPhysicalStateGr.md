# W3_IsForwardPredictionEnoughPhysicalStateGr — Is Forward Prediction Enough? Physical State Grounding for JEPA World Models (PSG-JEPA) (2026, arXiv id not in text)
Setup: LeWM JEPA backbone (encoder + action-conditioned predictor, 3 context frames, single-step prediction, windows of 4), trained per-environment; added training-only heads: state grounding (latent → proprio) and transition grounding (latent pair → multi-horizon joint-angle change Δq, k∈{1,2,3}), λg=0.1. Eval: probes and GC-IDM planning on OGBench Cube/Scene (200 goals, 3 planner seeds); policy learning on LIBERO-Goal with encoder fine-tuned + OFT-style 4-layer MLP head (2 frames, chunk 8), 3 seeds × 500 rollouts; real AgileX Cobot (Mobile-ALOHA design), 3 RGB cams (front + 2 wrist), 3 tasks, 100 teleop demos/task, 50 trials/task.
Claim: forward prediction alone leaves robot state and state change poorly decodable from latents; adding proprio + joint-change grounding heads during training fixes this and improves planning and policy learning without inference cost.
Evidence:
 - Probes: EE-yaw MLP r: LeWM ≤0.11, DINOv2 0.50, PSG 0.98. Transition probes (linear/MLP): gripper vel LeWM 0.44/0.47, DINOv2 0.20/0.12, PSG 0.69/0.76.
 - Planning OGBench-Cube full data: PSG 95.0% at 5 GC-IDM epochs vs LeWM 80.7% (LeWM 89.7% at 100 epochs); 5% data PSG 84.5% (~5× data efficiency). OGBench-Scene full data 100 epochs: PSG 96.2, LeWM-ActionIDM 94.7, LeWM 91.0, DINOv2 90.2.
 - LIBERO-Goal policy: PSG 85.3% vs LeWM-ActionIDM 82.6, DINOv2-base (fine-tuned) 80.1, LeWM 77.7.
 - Real (50 trials): Place-to-Bread 84 vs 62, Place-to-Plate 74 vs 58, Pour-Water 80 vs 60; avg 79.3 vs 60.0 (PSG vs LeWM only).
 - Open-loop latent rollout MSE at 30 model steps: Cube 0.1488 → 0.0485; Scene 0.1608 → 0.0982.
Ablations (Table 5): w/o transition grounding: planning 95.0 → 81.3; w/o state grounding: state-probe r 0.94 → 0.69; any removal → LIBERO-Goal 80–81.5 vs 85.3 (differences among ablations within seed noise); multi-horizon vs adjacent/endpoint only: planning 95.0 vs 93.5/93.7, policy 85.3 vs 81.5.
Failure/limitations: Critical read: real comparison only against the same LeWM backbone, not against a standard ImageNet/DINOv2 policy or ACT/DP; encoders trained per-environment (small JEPA, parameter count not stated); no robustness tests (lighting/camera shift). Inverse-dynamics (action) auxiliary is a strong, simpler alternative (82.6 vs 85.3).
Conflicts: supports the idea that auxiliary objectives tying visual latents to robot state/dynamics help in low-data regimes (cf. inverse-dynamics aux losses, MCR, RS-CL); disagrees with "just use frozen DINOv2" — frozen DINOv2 poorly encodes gripper/orientation state (probes) and underperforms here. The LaST0 note found future-proprio latents also help — consistent direction.
Relevance: moderate. A cheap recipe for our small policy: add training-only heads predicting current joint state and Δq over 1–3 steps from the visual latent (we have proprio for free). Real result (100 demos/task, 3 cams incl. wrists, pick-place) is close to our regime.
Decision impact:
 - Q09 auxiliary objectives: proprio-state + multi-horizon joint-change grounding heads: real 60.0 → 79.3% avg (50 trials × 3 tasks, 100 demos) vs forward-prediction-only JEPA; inverse-dynamics aux also helps (77.7 → 82.6 LIBERO) — confidence M.
 - Q09: pure forward (future latent) prediction is not sufficient on its own — confidence M.
 - Q03 vision encoder: fine-tuned DINOv2-base 80.1 vs grounded JEPA 85.3 on LIBERO-Goal; frozen DINOv2 poorly encodes gripper velocity/EE yaw — weakens frozen generic encoders without state grounding — confidence L (sim).
