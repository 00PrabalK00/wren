# W3_CapturingVisualEnvironmentStructureCorre — Capturing Visual Environment Structure Correlates with Control Performance (2026, arXiv 2602.04880, ICLR 2026)
Setup: 9 pretrained encoders (ResNet-18-IN, ViT-B-IN, CLIP, MoCo-v3, MAE, DINOv1, DINOv2, R3M, Stable Diffusion; appendix + VC-1, LIV), mostly FROZEN, under a multi-task language-conditioned (CLIP text, CFG) diffusion policy; also MLP policy (appendix). Sim: MetaWorld-50, RoboCasa (24 tasks), SimplerEnv (6 Google + 4 WidowX); 50 demos/task; 100 rollouts/task. Real: xArm6 + corner RealSense D455, 10 Hz, 2 tasks (carrot-on-plate, stack cube), 100 demos/task, 100 rollouts, 5 backbones. Compute: A100.
Claim: a linear probe predicting full simulator state (object pose/shape/material, joint angles, EE pose, lighting) from frozen features ranks encoders by downstream policy success better than segmentation/depth/ImageNet/few-shot proxies, and adding it as an auxiliary loss improves policies.
Evidence:
 - No universally best frozen encoder (Fig 3, figure only, qualitative): MetaWorld (cartoonish) favors ImageNet ResNet/ViT, CLIP/DINOv2 underperform; RoboCasa favors DINOv1/DINOv2 (object localization); SimplerEnv (real-like) favors R3M.
 - MetaWorld frozen, 5 runs x 5000 rollouts (Table II): ResNet-IN 0.629, ViT-IN 0.650, MAE 0.626, CLIP 0.562 (SEM <=0.004).
 - Proxy quality (Table 1, avg over 4 envs): Ours MMRV 0.035 / r 0.753 vs segmentation 0.105 / -0.069, depth ~0.096 / -0.071, few-shot 0.068 / 0.347, action-MSE 0.089 / -0.294. Real world: correlation of sim-proxy with real success comparable to sim (Fig 6, figure only).
 - Fine-tuning (A.1): all backbones improve over frozen but unequally — DINOv2 improves a lot, MoCo-v3 minimally; ranking changes after fine-tuning (backbone lr 1e-6).
Ablations: joint auxiliary state-prediction loss during fine-tuning (MetaWorld, Table VIII) direct FT -> joint -> two-stage: ViT-IN 0.683/0.740/0.722; MoCo-v3 0.671/0.743/0.719; MAE 0.648/0.712/0.680; CLIP 0.765/0.801/0.788; DINOv2 0.767/0.795/0.784. CLIP over epochs: direct FT saturates ~0.77 at 20+ epochs; joint reaches 0.811 at 30. Diffusion policy > 3-layer MLP policy (A.2, qualitative). State groups matter differently per env: MetaWorld/RoboCasa need 2D object shape/localization; SimplerEnv needs 3D EE pose and object orientation (Table 2).
Failure/limitations: aux loss needs simulator ground-truth state (appendix: detector-estimated real states still correlate, slightly worse); fine-tuned results only on MetaWorld; real-world only 2 tasks, 5 backbones, results figure-only; mostly frozen backbones, which is not our regime.
Conflicts: agrees with Burns et al. / "what makes pretrained reps good" that no single pretraining wins everywhere; disagrees that segmentation-style proxies are general. Fine-tuned CLIP/DINOv2 (~0.77-0.80) beat fine-tuned ImageNet ViT (0.68) — consistent with VLA practice of SigLIP/DINOv2 encoders — while frozen CLIP is worst in MetaWorld (0.562), consistent with reports that frozen CLIP features are poor for control.
Relevance: for our real-scene pick-place, suggests (a) fine-tune rather than freeze, (b) DINOv2 / CLIP-family backbones gain most from fine-tuning, (c) an auxiliary head predicting cheap state labels we already have (joint angles, EE pose; pumpkin pose from RealSense depth/segmentation) is a low-cost regularizer (+3-7 pts in sim). Small encoders (ResNet-18) are competitive when frozen.
Decision impact:
 - Q03 vision encoder: supports fine-tuned DINOv2/CLIP over ImageNet ViT (MetaWorld FT 0.767/0.765 vs 0.683), and "no universal frozen winner" — M (100-rollout sim, many tasks; real only figure).
 - Q09 auxiliary objectives: supports joint state-prediction aux loss (+2.8 to +7.2 pts across 5 backbones, joint > two-stage) — M (sim only, consistent across backbones).
 - Q01 action head: diffusion > MLP regression policy under same encoders — L (appendix, figure-only).
