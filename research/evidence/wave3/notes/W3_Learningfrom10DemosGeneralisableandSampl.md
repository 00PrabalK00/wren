# W3_Learningfrom10DemosGeneralisableandSampl — Learning from 10 Demos: Generalisable and Sample-Efficient Policy Learning with Oriented Affordance Frames (2025, arXiv 2410.12124)
Setup: real Franka Panda, Cartesian impedance control, GELLO teleop; 10 demos per sub-task; tea-serving task decomposed into 7 sub-tasks. State-based Diffusion Policy (8.08M params, 16-step chunk, execute 8, 4500 epochs). State = tool pose in an "oriented affordance frame" + gripper + object rotation (6D rot). Actions = 16 future EE poses in the same frame + gripper + a self-progress scalar. Frames come from ArUco markers (main results) or a marker-free pipeline (GroundingDINO + SAM + image-to-3D mesh + FoundationPose ~20 Hz on an RTX 4090 + DINO feature matching). About 11 trials per condition (percentages are multiples of 1/11), 10 trials for the perception test.
Claim: expressing state/actions in an object-affordance frame oriented towards the tool gives spatial and intra-category generalization from 10 demos, and a progress output lets sub-policies be chained.
Evidence (Table 1, avg over 7 sub-tasks, 10 demos):
 - In-distribution: oriented affordance frame 90.9% vs end-effector frame 48.1% vs global frame 59.7%.
 - Spatial OOD (objects moved to other workspace regions): 83.1% vs EE frame 41.6% vs global 0%. Composites: 72.7% vs 6.1%; full 7-step tea serving: 63.6% vs 9.1%.
 - Image-based DP (RGB, like Chi et al.) on cup rearrangement: fails completely with 10 demos and needs 305 demos to match 81.8% (~30x more data; figure).
 - Intra-category: 8/10 unseen teacups and 3/3 unseen teapots succeed.
Ablations:
 - Non-oriented affordance frame: roughly half the success at 10 demos and never above 80% even at 50 demos; oriented frame reaches 100% at 30 demos (figure). Table 2: oriented 82% vs non-oriented 46%, with the non-oriented failures being joint-limit violations (36.4%).
 - Marker-free perception vs ArUco: 7/10 vs 8/10 (one extra failure from tracking loss under robot occlusion).
Failure/limitations: needs a human-specified affordance (3 reference points + object name) per task and reliable 6D object tracking. Tracking breaks under occlusion and for deformable/articulated objects. Heavy perception stack (FoundationPose, image-to-3D). Small trial counts (~11). The image-DP comparison is a single task and uses the baseline's default recipe (no augmentation or pretrained encoder details given).
Conflicts: consistent with other object-centric/relative-frame results (e.g. NDF, cable-routing gripper frame), which all find that removing absolute position from the problem is the biggest lever at <=50 demos. It conflicts in spirit with end-to-end RGB policies (ACT/DP) that reach good ID success at 50 demos, but their spatial OOD is exactly where those fail. The 305-demo figure is the strongest "structure beats data" number in this set.
Relevance: medium. Our pumpkin->tray task is a 2-object pick-place that fits the affordance-frame recipe: pumpkin pose from the RealSense RGB-D (segmentation + centroid would be enough for a roughly symmetric pumpkin) and the tray pose. A state-based DP in an object-relative frame is tiny (8M params) and runs well within 8 GB. It is also immune to lighting and appearance changes as long as the detector works, which moves the robustness burden onto an open-vocabulary detector/segmenter. The costs are a perception pipeline, loss of closed-loop visual feedback during the grasp, and the SO-101's IK precision (backlash). A hybrid (object-relative position features as extra inputs to an RGB policy) is the lower-risk variant.
Decision impact:
 - Q02 action space: + object-relative (affordance-frame) EE targets vs global/EE frame: OOD 83.1% vs 0%/41.6% — confidence M (real, ~11 trials, marker-based)
 - Q04 3D / structured state: + a 3D object pose from RGB-D + foundation models as policy input gives spatial and instance generalization from 10 demos — confidence M
 - Q13 data quantity: + image DP needed 305 demos to match 10 structured demos (single task) — confidence L-M
 - Q14 robustness: + novel instances 8/10 cups, 3/3 teapots via the perception pipeline — confidence L (small n)
