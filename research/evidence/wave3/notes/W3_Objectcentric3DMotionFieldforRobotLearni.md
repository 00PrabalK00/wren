# W3_Objectcentric3DMotionFieldforRobotLearni — Object-centric 3D Motion Field for Robot Learning from Human Videos (2025, arXiv 2506.04227)
Setup: learns only from RGB-D human videos (RealSense D435, 640x480 at 30 Hz, object ~40–50 cm away, 50–150 videos per task collected in 2–15 min); no robot data, no wrist cam. A UNet "denoising" 3D motion-field estimator is trained on 8M synthetic ShapeNet depth/flow samples with simulated depth noise (data generated in 1 day on 8 L40s; estimator trained ~1 day on 16 A100-40GB). The policy (UNet, Gaussian or diffusion head) predicts a dense object 3D motion field from segmented (SAM2) RGB-D. Actions come from Kabsch + RANSAC on the object points (object assumed already grasped). XArm7; 5 tasks (pick-rotate-place, line tracking, push tool, wrench, 2.5 mm insertion).
Claim: an object-centric 3D motion field recovered robustly from noisy depth is a better video action representation than pixel flow or raw point-cloud flow.
Evidence: >50% lower 3D motion estimation error than the direct (raw depth + tracker) method. ~55% average zero-shot real success vs General Flow <=10% (figure only, 3 seeds).
Ablations: fine-grained insertion (Table 1): full 35.0%; w/o diffusion head (Gaussian) 0.0%; w/o masked diffusion 0.0%; w/o object-masking augmentation 5.0%. Intrinsics map (inverse focal + pixel coords) is crucial, even for ~10° FoV variation (figure).
Failure/limitations: assumes grasp and segmentation succeed (trials restarted otherwise). Insertion shows bang-bang corrections. Uses a heavy offline pipeline.
Conflicts: supports "diffusion head > unimodal Gaussian regression for precision" (like ACT's CVAE ablation) even when demos are consistent. Raw RealSense depth is too noisy for direct 3D motion without learned denoising, which is a caution for point-cloud policies built on D435-class depth.
Relevance: low-medium. The takeaways are (a) RealSense depth noise is a real problem for fine geometry and needs filtering or learned denoising before we rely on depth/point clouds; (b) object-centric segmented input gave background robustness (qualitative); (c) a generative head mattered for precision.
Decision impact:
 - Q01 action head: + diffusion vs Gaussian head for precise motion 35% vs 0% — confidence L-M (one fine-grained task; trial count not stated)
 - Q04 depth: ~ D435 depth needs SDK temporal/spatial filters and learned denoising for accurate 3D motion; raw depth-based flow is error-prone — confidence M
 - Q14 robustness: + object-centric segmented input -> background robustness (qualitative only) — confidence L
