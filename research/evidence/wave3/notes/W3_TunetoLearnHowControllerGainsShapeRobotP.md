# W3_TunetoLearnHowControllerGainsShapeRobotP — Tune to Learn: How Controller Gains Shape Robot Policy Learning (2026, arXiv id not in text; Improbable AI Lab)
Setup: BC study in sim with state-matched datasets across a Kp x Kd grid via "Torque-to-Position Retargeting" (record torque demos at 500 Hz, convert to position targets for any gains, replay at 50 Hz, keep successes). 6 tasks (bimanual handover, dishrack load/unload, dishwasher open, mug hang, block stack), 100 teleop demos/task (Vision Pro), nominal policy = VAE + MLP, obs history 10, chunk 10, privileged state input, absolute joint actions; 100 rollouts per gain cell. Ablations over regression/VAE/diffusion, MLP/Transformer, chunk 1 vs 10, absolute vs relative joints, 10 vs 50 Hz, dataset size, state vs dual RGB (global + wrist). Real: Franka FR3 end-to-end BC (100 teleop demos per gain corner, 400 total), 12-user teleop study (1,297 trials), RL sim2real reaching (30 real rollouts per gain).
Claim: gains should be chosen for learnability, not desired task compliance: BC prefers compliant (low Kp) + overdamped (high Kd); RL works with any gains if hyperparameters retuned; sim2real is hurt by stiff+overdamped.
Evidence:
 - BC heatmaps (Fig 5, 7 settings): success concentrates in low-Kp/high-Kd corner in all tasks; logistic regression with 100 rollouts/cell: lower Kp and higher Kd significant; Barnard's test rejects H0 in all 6 tasks (p << 0.0083). Exact cell values only partially legible (e.g. handover grid values ranging ~0.05–0.50).
 - Real FR3 end-to-end (Fig 11): compliant-overdamped 71%, compliant-underdamped 50%, stiff-overdamped 8%, stiff-underdamped 5% (trial count not stated in main text).
 - Teleop user study: with per-gain tuned input scaling, compliant-overdamped gives comparable or better success, time and rating (figure only).
 - Compliant gains give HIGHER train/val MSE but HIGHER closed-loop success; open-loop replay with injected action noise: compliant keeps success, stiff "completely fail" (Fig 8, figure only).
 - Sim2real RL: SysID error >10x lower for stiff-overdamped, yet closed-loop sim2real trajectory error largest there (high-frequency oscillation, persists with DR). Jitter-failure fraction by policy rate: 100 Hz 21.8%, 50 Hz 20.2%, 20 Hz 7.4%, 10 Hz 5.0%.
Ablations: preference for compliant/overdamped holds across dataset size, regression/VAE/diffusion, MLP/Transformer, chunk 1 vs 10, absolute vs relative joint actions, 10 vs 50 Hz (Figs 23–27, figure only); compliant/overdamped also shows steeper data scaling (Fig 28, figure only). Task-level stiffness is set by learned reactions: a policy on 32x lower actuator stiffness achieved ~5x higher effective stiffness.
Failure/limitations: BC grid is sim with privileged states (RGB ablation exists but only for block stack in appendix); real BC result is one task, 4 corners, trial count unreported; Franka torque-controlled impedance loop at 1 kHz differs from hobby servo firmware PID. Critical read: mechanism (controller low-pass filters policy prediction errors/jitter) is plausible and general; retargeting ensures a clean causal comparison.
Conflicts: Contradicts implicit default in DROID/OXE (stiff tracking). Consistent with ACT's leader-follower setup where the leader–follower gap encodes force (compliant follower). "Lower val loss ≠ better policy" agrees with DP/other reports that BC validation loss is a poor checkpoint selector.
Relevance: HIGH and cheap for SO-101. Feetech STS3215 servos expose P/I/D coefficients (LeRobot already lowers P for SO-100/101 to reduce shakiness). This paper says: for BC, keep the follower compliant (lower P) and well-damped (higher D) both during data collection and deployment, same gains for both; expect smoother rollouts and more forgiveness of chunk-boundary jumps and jitter. Also: judge checkpoints by rollouts, not val loss. Lower policy rate reduces oscillation (sim2real RL finding, only weakly transferable to BC).
Decision impact:
 - Q02 action space: absolute position targets are fine; the gains under them matter more — compliant+overdamped preferred for BC regardless of abs/rel — confidence M (sim grid, significant tests; one real task 71% vs 5–8%).
 - Q10 latency/smoothness: compliant, overdamped low-level control attenuates action errors/jitter; lower policy rate reduces oscillation (21.8% @100 Hz -> 5.0% @10 Hz, RL sim2real) — confidence M.
 - Q13 data: compliant/overdamped gains give steeper BC data scaling; teleop with compliant gains is not harder if input mapping tuned — confidence L-M (figure only).
 - Q06 chunking: preference holds for chunk 1 and 10; no chunk-size conclusion — confidence L.
