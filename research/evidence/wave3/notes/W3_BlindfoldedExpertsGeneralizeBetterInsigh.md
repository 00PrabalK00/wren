# W3_BlindfoldedExpertsGeneralizeBetterInsigh — Blindfolded Experts Generalize Better: Insights from Robotic Manipulation and Videogames (2025, NeurIPS 2025; arXiv id not in text)
Setup: Real: Franka Panda peg insertion (FMB-style, 10 peg shapes), 2 wrist-mounted D405 cameras, board inside black bin; 400 human teleop trajectories per training shape (!), from random initial pose; train on k∈{2,3,4,5} shapes, test on rest; policy = frozen ImageNet ResNet-10 (shared across 2 wrist views) + proprio MLP → single GRU → Gaussian policy; 24 insertion attempts per shape. Sim: Procgen maze/heist, 200 levels × 20 human trajectories, ResNet+GRU, 10 seeds.
Claim: if the demonstrator is deprived of task-identifying information ("blindfold": SAM2 masks the hole shape in the teleop view; or pixel noise), the demos become exploratory/searching, and cloning them (with a history-based policy, observations recorded unmasked) generalizes much better to unseen tasks; theory: generalization gap ∝ sqrt(I(task;expert obs)/m).
Evidence:
 - Table 2 (train on square+star, 400 demos per noise level; success on train square/star, test plus/ellipse): no blindfold 71/67 → 20/17; noise P=100: 100/88 → 96/79; P=170: 92/71 → 58/62; P=200 (demos only 58% successful): 46/38 → 21/54; SAM2 mask blindfold: 96/96 → 100/96.
 - Fig. 6 (k=2 and k=5 train shapes): BF-expert policy better than standard on all shapes including training shapes (figure only); gap larger with fewer training shapes.
 - Procgen maze: standard-expert BC overfits even with 2× more trajectories matched for total steps (πBC-ext), BF-expert BC keeps improving on test (figure only).
Ablations: blindfold strength: too little (0) no exploration, too much (P=200) degrades demo success and policy; SAM2 mask > noise. Matched-step control rules out "just more data".
Failure/limitations: authors: blindfold must be designed per domain; balance is unresolved. Critical read: real task is a search-friendly contact task (peg insertion where wiggling/searching is a valid strategy); 400 demos/shape — far more than our budget; memory (GRU) policy is required to exploit exploratory demos; benefit on training shapes attributed to robustness to lighting/control errors, not tested explicitly.
Conflicts: goes against the common practice of collecting clean, shortest-path demos (and against pause/idle trimming intuition) — here "less optimal", searching behaviour generalizes better. Consistent with DART/noise-injection and recovery-data literature: broader state coverage in demos → more robust closed-loop policy. Contrasts with S2I (mixed-quality) where noisy/jittery demos hurt ACT/DP — the difference: here the exploration is purposeful and task-completing, and the policy has recurrent memory.
Relevance: moderate. For pumpkin→tray, the analogue would be demos with natural corrective/searching motions (e.g., regrasp when the pumpkin slips, approach from different directions) rather than perfectly scripted paths; a teleop "blindfold" (noisy/degraded view for the operator) is a cheap way to induce such demos, but needs a history-aware policy. Our 50–100 demo budget is far below their 400/shape.
Decision impact:
 - Q13 data quality/diversity: supports purposefully exploratory/corrective demos over shortest-path demos for generalization to unseen object variants (test shapes 17–20% → 79–100%) — confidence M (real robot, 24 trials/shape, but 400 demos/shape, one task family).
 - Q07 history: exploiting exploratory demos requires a memory policy (GRU) — confidence L (stated, not ablated).
 - Q14 robustness: BF policy also better on training shapes (robust to lighting/control error) — confidence L (figure, attribution speculative).
