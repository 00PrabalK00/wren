# W3_SutureBotAPrecisionFrameworkBenchmarkFor — SutureBot: A Precision Framework & Benchmark For Autonomous End-to-End Suturing (2025, arXiv id not in text)
Setup: dVRK Si bimanual; stereo endoscope 960×540 + 2 wrist borescope cams 640×480, 30 Hz; 1,890 teleop demos over 3 subtasks (pickup 628, throw 310, knot 952) incl. 454 DAgger-style recovery demos; variation in joint configs, RCM, pad pose, needle pose, wrist-cam mount perturbations. Hierarchical: Swin-based high-level language task predictor + low-level policy (relative action chunks) conditioned on language + goal image overlay. Low-level: multitask language-conditioned ACT (from scratch) vs fine-tuned π0, GR00T N1, OpenVLA-OFT; trained on 8×A100; 10 full procedures per model (10 trials).
Claim: goal conditioning via point labels overlaid on the image improves insertion precision 59–74%; a small multitask ACT beats large VLAs on this out-of-pretraining-domain, precision task.
Evidence:
 - Goal format (throw success / insertion error mm): ACT point label 9/10, 1.3±0.9; distance map 8/10, 2.6; mask 10/10, 2.9; no goal 10/10, 3.2. π0 point 6/10, 1.0±1.3; map 2.1; mask 1.8; no goal 3.9±2.5.
 - Model comparison (pickup / throw / pull / knot, end-to-end): ACT 9/8/4/9, 3/10 E2E, insertion 1.5±0.8 mm, 182 s; π0 7/7/3/4, 0/10, 1.9 mm, 348 s; GR00T N1 1/2/1/1; OpenVLA-OFT 0/0/0/0. ACT > GR00T (p=8.8e-9), > OpenVLA (p=2.1e-12), vs π0 p=0.018 (not significant at corrected α).
 - Pretraining (π0 variants, pickup/throw/pull/knot): standard π0 7/7/3/4; π0 post-trained on 20K cholecystectomy traj 4/4/0/5; π0 from PaliGemma only (no robot pretraining) 6/2/2/1. Pretraining mainly sped convergence.
 - Generalization (pickup / throw / pull / knot): ACT trained wound 9/8/4/9 → unseen wounds 5/6/2/5, new lighting 3/5/7/4, swapped tools 1/9/1/2; π0 trained 7/7/3/4 → unseen wounds 5/6/0/?, lighting 0/5/0/?, tools 1/5/0/? (last column truncated in text). Both degrade strongly under lighting and tool changes; π0 more stable across wound geometry per authors.
 - MSE-trained π0/GR00T overfit quickly; ACT/OFT (L1) trained ≥10k steps; DP failed to reach reasonable performance in their implementation.
Ablations: goal-format ablation above; high-level policy vs human oracle ≈ equal (ACT oracle 7/7/5/10; π0 oracle 3/9/3/7).
Failure/limitations: authors: 10 trials per condition, manual target selection, lighting/tool changes break policies, need more diverse data. Critical read: statistical tests on n=10; ACT's advantage may come from hyperparameter tuning effort and the uniform dataset; no augmentation reported, which likely explains lighting failure.
Conflicts: agrees with ACT and other low-data papers that a from-scratch ~80M ACT can match/beat fine-tuned billion-parameter VLAs when the domain is far from pretraining data and data is uniform; conflicts with VLA papers claiming pretrained VLAs give robustness — here π0 collapses under lighting change too (pickup 7→0/10). Points to the SmolVLA lighting failure we observed being a data/augmentation problem, not solved by pretraining alone.
Relevance: high for Q12/Q14. Our situation (single task, ~50–100 uniform demos, lighting shift failures) mirrors this: small ACT is competitive; neither model is lighting-robust without diverse data/augmentation. Goal-point overlay on the image is a cheap conditioning trick (e.g., mark tray location) if we ever need placement precision.
Decision impact:
 - Q12 model size: small multitask ACT ≥ π0 (3B) ≫ GR00T N1, OpenVLA-OFT on real precision task with ~300–950 demos/subtask — supports small from-scratch models — confidence M (10 trials, but large margins vs GR00T/OFT).
 - Q14 robustness: lighting change breaks both ACT (pickup 9→3/10) and π0 (7→0/10); pretraining is no substitute for visual diversity/augmentation — confidence M.
 - Q13 data: recovery demos included (454/1890) but not ablated — L.
 - Q01 action head: L1 (ACT/OFT) needed ≥10k steps, MSE flow VLAs overfit fast; DP failed in their hands — ~ — L.
 - Q08 language/goal conditioning: image-overlaid point goals beat mask/distance-map channels (1.3 vs 2.6–2.9 mm for ACT) — confidence L-M.
