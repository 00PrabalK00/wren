# W3_ADualProcessVLAEfficientRoboticManipulat — A Dual Process VLA: Efficient Robotic Manipulation Leveraging VLM (DP-VLA) (2024, CoRL 2024 workshop-style paper; arXiv id not in text)
Setup: SIM ONLY. RoboCasa (MuJoCo, Franka), 24 atomic kitchen tasks, Generated-3000 set = 72,000 MimicGen-generated episodes (~3000/task). S-Sys1 = BC-Transformer (robomimic) trained from scratch, ResNet18 per image, 3 cams at 128x128 (left/right agentview + eye-in-hand) + proprio; L-Sys2 = OpenVLA-7B (single agentview image) whose latent 4096-D feature (computed ONLY at the first frame) conditions S-Sys1 in place of the CLIP language embedding. 7-D EEF delta actions. 1x RTX 6000 Ada. RoboCasa eval protocol (unseen kitchen styles + unseen object instances); 50 trials/task implied by 0.02 granularity (not stated explicitly).
Claim: a small fast policy conditioned on a (frozen) VLM latent beats both the fine-tuned VLA and the small policy alone, at near small-policy speed.
Evidence (Table 1, avg success over 24 tasks): OpenVLA-ft 0.098; BC-xfmr (CLIP lang) 0.476; DP-VLA 0.573. PnP tasks: OpenVLA-ft ~0.00 on all 8 PnP; BC-xfmr 0.06–0.44; DP-VLA 0.10–0.62. Doors: BC-xfmr 0.46–0.50 vs DP-VLA 0.42–0.84.
Latency (Table 2, mean over first 50 frames; column order in extraction ambiguous, logical reading): OpenVLA-ft ~0.253 s/step, BC-xfmr 0.022 s, DP-VLA 0.030 s (VLM only called once per episode).
Ablations:
 - Which VLM feature (Table 3, 200k iters, OpenVLA-ft): Mean-of-Text 0.533, End-of-Text 0.490, Start-of-Action 0.543, End-of-Action 0.520 — differences ~5 pts, likely within noise.
 - Pretrained vs fine-tuned VLM as feature source (Table 4, End-of-Text, 300k iters): OpenVLA-pt 0.556 > OpenVLA-ft 0.512 → fine-tuning the big VLM on the sim data hurt its features.
Failure/limitations: sim only, huge data (3000 demos/task) — nothing like our 50–100 regime; VLM feature computed once per episode so it is effectively a better task/goal embedding, not closed-loop perception. The fine-tuned OpenVLA baseline at 9.8% is suspiciously weak (authors blame real→sim domain gap), so "VLA loses" is not a fair comparison. No seeds/variances reported.
Conflicts: consistent with the hierarchical fast/slow line (Helix, GR00T N1, Hi-Robot) that small high-rate controllers do the motor work; the gain over BC-xfmr is mainly on tasks where CLIP language fails to disambiguate (open vs close door), i.e. a task-conditioning effect, not a perception-robustness effect.
Relevance: For our single-task pumpkin pick-place, language disambiguation is irrelevant; the latency point (keep the per-step network small, call any big model rarely) matches our 2 s SmolVLA problem. Not evidence about robustness to lighting/camera shift on a real robot.
Decision impact:
 - Q10 latency: supports small per-step policy + rarely-called large model (0.030 s vs ~0.25 s) — confidence L (sim, timing only).
 - Q12 model size: weakly supports small from-scratch policy over fine-tuned 7B VLA in-domain (0.476 vs 0.098) — confidence L (baseline likely mis-tuned, sim, 3000 demos/task).
 - Q08 language conditioning: VLM latent as task embedding beats CLIP text embedding (0.573 vs 0.476) for multi-task — confidence L (sim, multi-task only).
