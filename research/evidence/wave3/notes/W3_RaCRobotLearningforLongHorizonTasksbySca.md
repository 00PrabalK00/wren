# W3_RaCRobotLearningforLongHorizonTasksbySca — RaC: Robot Learning for Long-Horizon Tasks by Scaling Recovery and Correction (2025, arXiv 2509.07953)
Setup: Real bimanual 2x xArm-7 with soft grippers; 3 cams (overhead + 2 wrist), 128x128 RGB, 60 Hz; 3 real long-horizon tasks (shirt hanging 5 subtasks, airtight lid sealing 5, takeout-box packing 7) + 1 sim bimanual assembly. Policy: ~300–368M MM-DiT flow matching, separate ResNet-50 per camera (ResNet-18 in sim), chunk H=60 (1 s), 10 Euler steps, re-plan every 0.5 s (execute half chunk). Data: initial expert demos then K rounds (6/10/9) of human-in-the-loop interventions via VR clutch; RaC rule 1 = operator first REWINDS robot to an in-distribution state (recovery) then completes the subtask (correction); rule 2 = end episode right after intervention. Eval: 60 trials real, 100 sim.
Claim: explicitly collecting recover-then-correct intervention data scales imitation ~10x more data-efficiently than batched expert demos and beats HG-DAgger.
Evidence: shirt hanging full success 78.3% with ~5 h of RaC data vs ALOHA Unleashed 75.0% with ~89 h (5345 demos) and GR-3 ~63.6% with 116 h (cross-paper, not same hardware). Shirt-hanging batched-demo baseline: RaC ≥2x data efficiency and steeper scaling curve (figure only). Sim assembly successes: full demos 23/100, HG-DAgger 22/100, RaC 61/100 (Fig 11). Success correlates linearly with number of recoveries per successful trajectory (r = 0.714, 0.803, 0.877 across the 3 real tasks).
Ablations (sim, figure only): intervention composition — RaC keeps recovery:correction ≈1:1–1:2; HG-DAgger drifts to 1:3 → 1:10 (mostly correction). Rule 2 (stop episode after intervention) alone scales better than continuing rollout (HG-DAgger); rule 1+2 best.
Failure/limitations: requires a trained policy + intervention interface (VR clutch) and multiple train/collect rounds; bimanual long-horizon tasks — larger model and 60 Hz; cross-paper comparisons (Table 1) not controlled; batched baseline only scaled on one real task.
Conflicts: Agrees with DART/BDI (noise-injected recovery data) and HG-DAgger/IWR literature that off-distribution recovery data beats more clean demos; adds the nuance that recovery (return to familiar state) matters more than correction alone. Contrasts with "clean demos only" data-quality advice from some LeRobot/ACT practice.
Relevance: For SO-101 pick-place the leader arm is a natural intervention device: run the policy, take over with the leader when it fails (e.g., missed pumpkin grasp), move back to a familiar pre-grasp pose, then complete — log only the human segment. Cheap and directly addresses compounding error; the 0.5 s replan with 1 s flow chunk is also a sane default.
Decision impact:
 - Q13 data quality: supports adding human-in-the-loop recovery+correction data over more clean demos (sim 61/100 vs 23/100 full demos vs 22/100 HG-DAgger; real ≥2x data efficiency) — confidence M-H (real robot, 60 trials, 3 tasks)
 - Q06 chunking: 1 s chunk, execute 0.5 s, re-plan — worked at scale (not ablated) — confidence L
 - Q01 action head: flow matching with 10 Euler steps adequate for mixed expert+intervention data (not ablated) — confidence L
