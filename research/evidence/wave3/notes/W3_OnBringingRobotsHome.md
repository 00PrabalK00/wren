# W3_OnBringingRobotsHome — On Bringing Robots Home (Dobb·E) (2023, arXiv 2311.16098)
Setup: Hello Robot Stretch RE1 (mobile, 6D wrist), single iPhone camera mounted on the gripper (wrist-only; RGB 1280x720 + LiDAR depth 256x192, resized to 256x256), no scene camera. Demos collected with a hand-held "Stick" (same iPhone mount, iPhone odometry gives 6D actions) — ~24 demos/task (~5 min, ~91 s of data), starts on a 4x6/5x5 grid. Pretraining: HoNY dataset 5620 demos/13 h/22 homes, ResNet34 MoCo-v3 SSL ("HPR"). Policy: ResNet34 (RGB) + median-pooled depth -> 2 FC layers -> 7-D relative EE action (3 trans, 3 axis-angle rot, gripper), single step, NO chunking, NO history, MSE loss, per-axis normalized, full fine-tune 50 epochs lr 3e-5, ~20 min on RTX A4000. Policy at 3.75 Hz, executed synchronously on Intel NUC. Eval: 109 tasks in 10 homes, 10 trials each.
Claim: a tiny single-step MSE BC policy on a domain-pretrained ResNet34 with wrist RGB-D learns simple home tasks from ~24 demos: 81% average success, 102/109 tasks ≥50%.
Evidence: 81% over 109 tasks x 10 trials. By task group: Air fryer closing / cushion flipping 100%, 6D pick&place worst at 56%, knob turning 65%. Tasks with no wrist rotation skew higher; full 6D tasks flattest distribution. Weak negative correlation demo length vs success (r=-0.24, p=0.012). Long-horizon: muffin-in-toaster 1/10, cup-in-drawer 6/10 (failure = not releasing object).
Ablations (lab benchmark of 10 tasks; almost all numbers figure only):
 - Visual encoder: HPR (in-domain MoCo on 13 h home data) vs VC-1, MVP, R3M, ImageNet-1K: abstract says HPR ≥23% better than other foundation models; VC-1 closest but bimodal (either ~HPR or total failure per environment); IN-1K/R3M/MVP lower (figure only).
 - Depth vs RGB-only: depth "much better" in most settings (figure only); RGB-only fails near featureless surfaces (doors/drawers) at gripper-close timing. Counter-example: vertical blinds at night, camera facing dark window -> depth 2/10 vs no-depth 10/10 (OOD depth values). Shiny air fryer handle 0/10 (bad lidar depth + mirror reflection).
 - #demos: success increases monotonically and saturates (figure only, 5 tasks).
 - Demonstrator expertise: novices' first task 10% / 0% success, second task 70% / 90% (vs experts 100%/70%) -> demo quality improves quickly with practice.
 - Odometry: iPhone hardware odometry >> OpenSfM visual odometry for action labels when camera is close to surfaces (qualitative, open-loop replay).
 - No history: pick&place between two identical-looking shelves 0/10; adding a distinguishing red book 7/10 (state aliasing from single-frame wrist view).
Failure/limitations: strong shadows (robot arm shadow) cause erratic behavior; fixed by overhead light; iPhone low-light processing made day→night transfer work. Lidar depth fails on shiny/reflective and dark distant scenes. Single-step, no temporal context. Critical read: success rates are per-task 10 trials, tasks are ~5 s short-horizon, and most ablations are figure-only; the setup is wrist-only, relative-EE, mobile base — very different embodiment from SO-101 joint position control.
Conflicts: Low-data BC with plain MSE works here (unlike ACT's CVAE ablation) — because tasks are short, unimodal (demos from a grid, one expert strategy), and single-step at 3.75 Hz. Encoder: in-domain SSL > VC-1/R3M/MVP agrees with "generic robot representations are not a silver bullet" findings; but no DINOv2/SigLIP comparison. Depth helps, consistent with DP3/iDP3 claims, but they show the lidar-failure caveat also seen by RealSense users on reflective objects.
Relevance: Strong real-world evidence (1090 trials) that (a) wrist RGB-D with a very simple depth feature (median-pooled) helps gripper timing, (b) lighting shifts mostly OK but strong unseen shadows break policies — directly our lighting failure mode, (c) ~24 demos is enough for short simple tasks, so 50-100 demos for pumpkin pick-place is reasonable, (d) single-frame wrist-only policies suffer state aliasing; our scene cam mitigates. RealSense depth will have the same reflective/dark-scene OOD issues.
Decision impact:
 - Q04 depth: supports adding depth (even crude pooled depth) to RGB — M (large real eval, but ablation figure only; one strong counterexample where depth hurt)
 - Q03 encoder: supports in-domain-pretrained, fully fine-tuned small ResNet over frozen generic robot reps (R3M/MVP/VC-1) — M
 - Q13 data: ~24 demos sufficient for 5 s tasks; success saturates with more demos; demonstrator practice matters (10%→70%) — M
 - Q14 robustness: lighting mostly OK with fine-tuned encoder, unseen strong shadows break policies — M (qualitative anecdotes)
 - Q07 history: single-frame policies fail under state aliasing (0/10 vs 7/10) — L (one task)
 - Q01 action head: plain MSE single-step works for short unimodal tasks — L
