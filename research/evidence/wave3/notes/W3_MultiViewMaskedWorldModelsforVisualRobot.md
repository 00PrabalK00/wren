# W3_MultiViewMaskedWorldModelsforVisualRobot — Multi-View Masked World Models for Visual Robotic Manipulation (MV-MWM) (2023, ICML; arXiv 2302.02408)
Setup: RLBench sim (front + wrist cams, 96x96 RGB, no proprio/depth), DreamerV2-style RL with expert demos in replay + BC loss; representation = multi-view video MAE on conv features (8-layer ViT enc, dim 256) with view-masking (mask a whole random view) + reward prediction; world model on frozen features. Viewpoint-robust setting: front camera pose randomized per episode (weak/medium/strong), brightness/contrast aug. Real: zero-shot sim-to-real Franka FR3, RealSense D435, Pick Up Cup, 3 viewpoints x 20 cup positions, no calibration. IL variant: 100 scripted demos, keyframe (next-best-pose) BC.
Claim: multi-view masked reconstruction (predict a hidden view from others) yields representations that help multi-view, single-view, and viewpoint-randomized control.
Evidence:
 - Aggregate success (Fig. 4, figure-only): MV-MWM > MWM > MAE+WM / CLIP+WM (frozen ImageNet/web encoders non-zero but far lower) > TCN+WM (near 0 with front+wrist) in multi-view, single-view (front, trained with wrist as auxiliary) and viewpoint-robust control.
 - Real zero-shot sim-to-real with uncalibrated cameras (Table 1): MV-MWM 74.7% vs MWM 11.3% vs MAE+WM 7.1%. Qualitatively works with a hand-held shaking camera.
 - IL (BC, 100 demos, single front view): MV-MWM beats MWM by 14.35 pts; view-masking 57.92 -> 64.48%.
Ablations: removing view-masking degrades strongly (figure); video autoencoding improves sample efficiency; higher mask ratio (95% > 75% > 50%) better; training representations on physically randomized camera views >> using rotation/translation/brightness image augmentation for viewpoint robustness (Fig. 18, figure-only).
Failure/limitations: simple single-object short tasks; RL with dense shaped rewards in sim; real only one task; physically randomizing cameras in the real world is hard (authors note).
Conflicts: Contrasts with "use frozen large pretrained encoder" practice — in-domain multi-view MAE beats frozen CLIP/MAE; consistent with findings that 2D image augmentation does not buy true viewpoint robustness (need real viewpoint diversity or 3D). Viewpoint randomization during training is the key ingredient for camera-shift robustness.
Relevance: Our camera-shift failure: evidence that (a) training data must include varied scene-camera poses (move the RealSense between demo sessions) rather than relying on crop/rotate augmentation; (b) a cross-view reconstruction auxiliary (predict wrist view from scene view and vice versa) is a cheap aux objective to make features robust and allow single-view fallback. Small model (ViT dim 256) fits on 8 GB.
Decision impact:
 - Q11 cameras / viewpoint robustness: supports physically varied camera viewpoints in training over image-space augmentation; real uncalibrated 74.7% vs 11.3% — confidence M (real sim-to-real but 1 task, RL setting).
 - Q09 auxiliary objectives: supports multi-view masked reconstruction (view-masking) aux/pretraining; IL +14.35 pts over single-view MAE-style, view-masking 57.9->64.5 — M (sim).
 - Q05 augmentation: image-space rotation/translation/brightness aug is insufficient for viewpoint shift compared to real viewpoint diversity — L/M (figure-only).
 - Q03 vision encoder: in-domain trained MAE representations beat frozen CLIP/ImageNet-MAE — L (RL, 96x96, sim).
