# W3_ContrastiveActionImagePretrainingforVisu — Contrastive Action-Image Pre-training for Visuomotor Control (CAIP) (2026, arXiv 2606.17256)
Setup: Encoder pretraining: SigLIP2 ViT-L/16 image+text towers, text-conditioned attention pooling, SigLIP sigmoid contrastive loss against a 4-layer action transformer encoding 64-step (≈2 s) chunks of 42 hand keypoints (delta SE(3)); 32,129 h egocentric video (+88 h robot), 128 H100, batch 32,768. Downstream: FROZEN encoder, per-task policy = Qwen3.5-0.8B decoder trained from scratch + flow-matching head, no proprio; real Dexmate Vega bimanual + 22-DoF Sharpa hands, 3 cams (head stereo + 2 wrist, 640x360), 30 Hz, 200 demos/task (150 pour), 6 tasks, 12 trials each (partial credit for multi-stage). 32 H100, 12–15 h per task. Sim: ManiSkill2 Franka 3 tasks, 200 demos, 12 trials.
Claim: aligning images with future hand-action chunks from human video yields a better frozen encoder for manipulation than DINOv2/SigLIP/MVP/R3M/VC-1.
Evidence (Table 1, real avg success %, same frozen-encoder policy): R3M 17.4, VideoMAE 26.0, VC-1 26.7, Qwen3.5 ViT 34.0, MVP 42.0, DINOv2 42.0, SigLIP 42.4, SigLIP2 43.4, CAIP 76.0. Per-task variance large: DINOv2 pour 81.3 but fold 22.9; MVP soap 93.8 but pick fruits 2.1; SigLIP2 fold 4.2.
 - ManiSkill2 Franka (Table 5, avg): VC-1 36.1, R3M 44.4, MVP 44.4, DINOv2 69.4, SigLIP2 72.2, CAIP 77.8.
 - Robustness (Tables 2/3, 3 tasks avg; train under original conditions): original / extra-light (shadows) / dark: CAIP 81.3/51.4/43.1, MVP 52.1/5.6/17.4, Qwen ViT 36.1/22.2/24.3. Two distractors: CAIP 81.3 -> 52.8, MVP 52.1 -> 9.7, Qwen 36.1 -> 28.5.
Ablations:
 - Encoder scale (Table 4, 3 real tasks): ViT-B 47.9, ViT-L 81.3, SO400M 87.5.
 - Pretraining data fraction (ManiSkill2): 20% 50.0, 50% 61.1, 100% 77.8.
 - Direct action regression as pretraining objective (MLP or transformer decoder, L1/MSE/Huber) "did not produce useful representations" (no numbers).
Failure/limitations: 12 trials per task (each trial ~8 pts); frozen-encoder protocol only (no fine-tuned DINOv2/ResNet baselines, which is what most small policies use); huge compute; dexterous-hand bias; robustness experiments compare only 3 encoders and not DINOv2/SigLIP. Even the best encoder loses ~30–40 pts under light/dark changes and ~30 pts with 2 distractors — frozen pretrained features alone do not give lighting robustness.
Conflicts: DINOv2 ≈ SigLIP ≈ MVP (~42%) as frozen features here, whereas other papers find DINOv2 clearly best among generic encoders — differences in token strategy/policy (0.8B scratch decoder, no proprio). ViT-B << ViT-L contrasts with small-model results where ResNet18 fine-tuned works fine at 50 demos (ACT/DP) — here encoder is frozen, so capacity must be in pretraining.
Relevance: Moderate/indirect. CAIP itself is not usable on 8 GB with our embodiment (ViT-L frozen is OK for inference but policy was 0.8B). Key takeaways: (1) with a FROZEN encoder, the choice matters enormously and varies per task — prefer fine-tuning or at least evaluate 2 encoders; (2) generic encoders frozen are fragile to lighting (MVP 52 -> 6 with shadows) — lighting robustness needs augmentation/data, not just pretrained features; (3) text-conditioned pooling focuses attention on task-relevant objects (qualitative).
Decision impact:
 - Q03 vision encoder: frozen generic encoders (DINOv2/SigLIP/MVP ~42%) far below action-aligned pretraining (76%); frozen-encoder ranking task-dependent — confidence M (real, 6 tasks, 12 trials).
 - Q14 robustness: frozen encoders drop heavily under lighting (CAIP 81 -> 43 dark; MVP 52 -> 6 with shadows) and distractors (81 -> 53) when trained on clean data only — confidence M.
 - Q12 model size: encoder ViT-B 47.9 vs ViT-L 81.3 when frozen — confidence L-M (3 tasks, 12 trials).
 - Q09 aux objectives: action-chunk contrastive alignment > direct action regression as pretraining — confidence L (no numbers).
