# W3_GoalConditionedDualActionImitationLearni — Goal-conditioned dual-action imitation learning for dexterous dual-arm robot manipulation (GC-DA) (2022/2025, arXiv 2203.09749, IEEE T-RO)
Setup: Real dual-arm UR5 banana peeling (9 subtasks, each a SEPARATE model), stereo head camera 1280x720 + eye-tracked human gaze → foveated crops; F/T sensing; VR teleop, demos recorded at only 5 Hz; subtask demos repeated 10–20x per banana for non-destructive subtasks (tens to hundreds of minutes per subtask, Table II). Global action = transformer predicting goal state s_T then whole remaining trajectory (open-loop until action-type classifier switches); local action = reactive delta a_t = s_{t+1} − s_t from the foveated image conditioned on predicted relative goal. Eval: 15 bananas per method, per-subtask scores.
Claim: use trajectory (chunk-like, goal-conditioned) prediction for coarse transport and reactive step-wise delta actions from foveated vision for precise contact phases; goal conditioning improves precise reaching.
Evidence (Table IV mean subtask score, 15 bananas): (a) GC-DA 0.870; (b) no goal-cond global 0.701; (c) no GC local 0.736; (d) no GC both 0.663; (e) reactive global 0.691; (f) reactive + no GC 0.754; (g) trajectory for BOTH global & local 0.439; (h) dual-resolution (global image for global action) 0.696; (i) Diffusion Policy (CNN, foveated stereo input) — GraspBanana 0.889 but GraspNeck 0.111 (not run on later subtasks).
 - GraspNeck (precise reach): GC-DA 0.933, NoGC-global 0.667, reactive-global 0.400, all-trajectory 0.000, Diffusion Policy 0.111.
Ablations: trajectory for precise local phase → collapses (GraspNeck 0.000, mean 0.439); reactive for global transport → drops (mean 0.691, GraspNeck 0.400); removing goal-conditioning drops precise reaching (0.667–0.867) but not peeling.
Failure/limitations: highly bespoke (gaze tracking, foveation, per-subtask models, human-annotated switch labels, manual resets between subtasks); 5 Hz data; the "trajectory" policies here predict the ENTIRE remaining sub-trajectory open-loop, which is much longer than a typical 1–2 s receding-horizon chunk — so "trajectory unsuitable for precision" is really "open-loop long horizon unsuitable". Diffusion Policy baseline with default hyperparameters at 5 Hz is likely under-tuned.
Conflicts: Superficially contradicts ACT/Diffusion Policy chunking wins for precision; the reconciliation is horizon + closed-loop re-planning: ACT/DP re-query every few steps (with temporal ensembling), here the global trajectory is executed until a classifier fires. Agrees with the chunk-size ablations elsewhere that very long/near-open-loop chunks hurt reactive, precise phases (ACT dip at k≥200).
Relevance: For pumpkin grasping on SO-101, supports keeping execution horizon short (closed-loop) near the grasp; long open-loop chunks only for transport. Gaze/foveation is not practical for us; wrist camera plays the analogous "foveated" role.
Decision impact:
 - Q06 chunking: long open-loop trajectories fail in precise contact phases (GraspNeck 0.000 all-trajectory vs 0.933 reactive-local) — supports short execution horizon / closed-loop near contact — confidence L-M (15 trials, bespoke system, very long horizon)
 - Q02 action space: step-wise delta (reactive) for local precise phase + goal-conditioned absolute trajectory for transport; mixed evidence — confidence L
 - Q09 auxiliary objectives: predicting the goal state first (goal-conditioning) improves precise reaching (0.933 vs 0.667) — confidence L-M
 - Q01 action head: default Diffusion Policy failed precise GraspNeck (0.111) at 5 Hz — confidence L (likely confounded by horizon/data rate)
