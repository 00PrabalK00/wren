# W3_TransporterNetworksRearrangingtheVisualW — Transporter Networks: Rearranging the Visual World for Robotic Manipulation (2020/22, arXiv 2010.14406, CoRL 2020)
Setup: Ravens-10 sim (PyBullet) with SE(2) pick/place motion primitives on a top-down orthographic RGB-D heightmap fused from 3 calibrated cameras; fully-convolutional pick (attention) + place (cross-correlation of rotated pick-crop features) networks; demos 1/10/100/1000 from scripted (partly stochastic) oracles; real UR5e kit assembly (suction, PhoXi depth, 8141 transitions) and sweeping (Azure Kinect, 6759 pushes) from 13 remote teleoperators.
Claim: preserving spatial equivariance (dense per-pixel action values, template matching of the picked region) makes vision-based pick-and-place orders of magnitude more sample-efficient than end-to-end regressors, and naturally represents multimodal place distributions.
Evidence: Simplified translation-only insertion (Table 3, 1/10/100/1000 demos): deterministic oracle — Transporter 100 at all; Conv-MLP 7/73/81/69. Stochastic (multimodal) oracle — Transporter 100 at all; Form2Fit 30/45/45 (at 10–1000); Conv-MLP 11/31/52/68; GT-state MLP 21/77/100/100. 6-DoF insertion: Transporter SE(3) 38/76/84/91 vs GT-state MLP 3-step 0/1/1/5. Real: kit assembly 98.9%, sweeping 98.3% test success (Table 5).
Ablations: stochastic vs deterministic demonstrators (above) — regression baselines degrade with inconsistent demos; spatially-structured multimodal outputs do not.
Failure/limitations (authors): sensitive to camera-robot calibration; primitive-based (not continuous closed-loop control); unclear how to add force/torque; real results lack variation tests.
Conflicts: consistent with ACT/Diffusion Policy/BDI evidence that unimodal regression struggles on multimodal (inconsistent) human demos, here via a dense heatmap head instead of a generative chunk head. Represents the "structured action space" alternative to end-to-end joint-space BC: extreme sample efficiency (1–10 demos) but depends on calibrated 3D top-down input and scripted motion primitives.
Relevance: Our pumpkin→tray task is literally a single pick-and-place; a Transporter-style pick/place point predictor on a RealSense heightmap + IK-scripted SO-101 motions would likely need only ~10 demos and be less sensitive to lighting (depth-dominated) but more sensitive to camera bumps (calibration) — the opposite trade-off of end-to-end BC. Not applicable to the continuous teleop-style policy we currently train, and SO-101 backlash limits scripted-IK precision.
Decision impact:
 - Q01 action head: dense spatial (heatmap/argmax) action heads handle multimodal demos where MLP regression fails (stochastic oracle 100% vs Conv-MLP 11–68%) — confidence M (sim, primitives)
 - Q04 3D vs RGB: top-down RGB-D heightmap with calibrated fusion gives 1–10-demo pick-place (sim) and 98–99% real — confidence L-M (primitive action space, calibration-sensitive)
 - Q13 data quantity: with spatial equivariance, 1–10 demos suffice for pick-place (sim) — confidence L for our end-to-end setting
