# W3_DecomposingtheGeneralizationGapinImitati — Decomposing the Generalization Gap in Imitation Learning for Visual Robotic Manipulation (2023, arXiv 2307.03659)
Setup: Real: Everyday Robots mobile manipulator, head camera, RT-1 (EfficientNet-B3 ImageNet + USE language, FiLM, TokenLearner, 8-layer transformer, 256-bin discrete actions, 6-frame history, 300x300), trained on 115K demos (13 skills, 35.2% in-domain), evaluated on 6 "pick X" tasks; 12 trials per factor value (2 per task), 1440 real evals total. Sim: "Factor World" (MetaWorld + 11 factors), pick-place, bin-picking, door-open; scripted experts; small 4-layer CNN + MLP BC from 84x84, 5 seeds; 5-100 training environments.
Claim: environment factors have a consistent difficulty ordering; camera pose and table texture are hardest, background/lighting/distractors easiest; random crop helps even non-spatial factors; out-of-domain robot data improves robustness but pretrained R3M/CLIP do not.
Evidence (real, crop+photometric aug policy):
 - Original 91.7%; new background 88.9%; new lighting 83.3%; new distractors 80.6%; new table texture 52.8%; new camera pose 45.8% (dataset used fixed head pose).
 - Pairs do not compound: texture+background 55.6%, texture+distractors 50.0% vs texture alone 52.8%. Sim: 16/21 factor pairs within +-6% of the harder single factor.
 - Sim: generalization gap for all factors shrinks from max ~0.4 to <0.1 going from 5 to 100 training environments. Object texture about as hard as camera position; table position (incl. height) as hard as table texture.
 - Continuous factors (sim): linearly increasing camera/table position range radius (0.025 -> 0.05 -> 0.075 m) roughly doubles the gap each step.
 - Data: in-domain-only vs with out-of-domain (full) — comparable on original tasks, but full dataset more robust across all shifts (Fig 8, figure only). Same-size subsample with out-of-domain ~ in-domain-only except new distractors 44.4% vs 75.0% (in-domain better).
Ablations: augmentation (real Fig 6, figure only, qualitative): random crop improves many factors, most on camera pose and table texture (non-spatial!); photometric distortion helps texture factors (table texture real; object/table/background sim). Pretrained frozen R3M / CLIP (ResNet-50) in sim: good in-distribution but large generalization gap; CLIP ~ scratch CNN except better on new object textures; R3M fails on most factors except distractors.
Failure/limitations: authors: few tasks/factors; training-env success drops slightly with more varied data (small CNN capacity). Critical read: real policy trained on 115K demos — far from our 50-100 regime; 12 trials/factor; lighting changes were colored LEDs (moderate); pretrained reps tested frozen with a small head, not fine-tuned.
Conflicts: agrees with "Data Scaling Laws in IL" and GenAug-style work that environment diversity dominates; contrasts with claims that frozen pretrained reps (R3M) give robustness — here they don't. Agrees with iDP3 that camera shift is the hardest factor for 2D policies.
Relevance: very high for our three failure modes: (1) moved camera is the single hardest factor (45.8%) -> keep scene cam fixed/rigidly mounted, randomize small camera pose in data, use random crop; (2) lighting was among the easiest (83.3%) when training has photometric aug + diverse data — our lighting failure suggests our data lacks diversity/aug; (3) new object appearance (object texture) is as hard as camera shift -> need object variety in demos (several pumpkins) or photometric/texture aug. Factors don't compound, so we can fix them one at a time.
Decision impact:
 - Q14 robustness: ordering camera pose (45.8%) < table texture (52.8%) < distractors (80.6%) < lighting (83.3%) < background (88.9%) — M/H (real 12 trials/cell + consistent sim, 5 seeds).
 - Q05 augmentation: supports random crop/shift (helps camera AND texture) + photometric jitter (texture factors) — M (real figure only; sim consistent).
 - Q11 cameras: fixed camera pose critical; gap ~doubles per +2.5 cm radius of camera shift — M (sim).
 - Q13 data: supports visual diversity over count (5->100 envs closes gap 0.4 -> <0.1; out-of-domain robot data improves robustness) — M.
 - Q03 vision encoder: frozen R3M/CLIP do not close generalization gap vs scratch CNN — L/M (sim, frozen only).
