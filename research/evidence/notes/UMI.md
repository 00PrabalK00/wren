# UMI — Universal Manipulation Interface: In-The-Wild Robot Teaching Without In-The-Wild Robots (2024, arXiv 2402.10329)
Setup: hand-held 3D-printed gripper with wrist GoPro (155° fisheye, side mirrors), IMU; EE pose from ORB-SLAM3 (modified: map-as-init, fiducial-marker init), gripper width from finger fiducials. Deployment: UR5e (+ Franka FR2 for cross-embodiment) with WSG-50 + TPU soft fingers, ONLY the wrist GoPro as camera, streamed via HDMI capture. Policy: Diffusion Policy (CNN U-Net head) with CLIP ViT-B/16 (ViT-L/14 for in-the-wild) fine-tuned at 10× lower LR; DDIM 16 inference steps; 10 Hz for quasi-static tasks (20 Hz tossing); image aug RandomCrop 0.95, Rotation ±5°, ColorJitter (b 0.3, c 0.4, s 0.5, h 0.08). Demos: cup arrangement 305 (2 demonstrators), tossing 280, cloth folding 250, dish washing 258 (1 demonstrator), in-the-wild cups 1400 across 30 locations, 15 cups (18 train styles purchased). Eval: 20 episodes per method with pixel-matched initial states (tossing counted per object, 120).
Claim: a hand-held gripper + careful policy interface (latency matching, relative EE trajectories, wide FoV wrist cam) transfers human demos to robots, and in-the-wild data yields policies that generalize to unseen environments/objects.
Evidence:
 - Cup arrangement (in-lab): 20/20; same checkpoint on Franka FR2 18/20 (failures = joint limits).
 - Dynamic tossing: 105/120 = 87.5%. Cloth folding bimanual: 14/20. Dish washing (7 steps, 258 demos incl. explicit recovery demos when ketchup is re-added): 14/20; robust to distractors, other sauces, lighting changes, base motion — qualitative/video.
 - In-the-wild cup (1400 demos, 30 envs, ViT-L): unseen cafe + water fountain: training cups 28/40 = 70%, unseen cups 15/20 = 75%, total 43/60 = 71.7%. Same ViT-L trained only on in-lab narrow data: 0% (robot doesn't even move toward cup).
 - Throughput (15 min, incl. resets): UMI >3× faster than SpaceMouse teleop on cup arrangement, 48% of bare-hand speed; tossing: UMI 64% of hand speed, teleop 0 successful demos.
 - SLAM accuracy vs MoCap: ATE 6.1 mm / 3.5°; inter-gripper RPE 10.1 mm.
Ablations:
 - Action space (cup, 20 trials): relative trajectory (w.r.t. current EE pose at chunk start) 20/20; delta (step-to-step, accumulates) 16/20; absolute (needs SLAM↔robot calibration) 5/20 (calibration bias).
 - No fisheye (rectify/crop to 69° FoV ≈ RealSense D415 HFoV): 11/20 — failures even when object visible, jittery (policy forced multimodal by poor visibility).
 - Side mirrors: raw mirror pixels 17/20 < no mirror 18/20 < digitally re-flipped mirrors 20/20.
 - No latency matching (tossing): 105/120 → 69/120; visibly jittery; gripper-release timing mismatch.
 - No inter-gripper proprioception (folding): 14/20 → 6/20.
 - Scratch ResNet-34 instead of CLIP ViT-B (dish washing): 0/10 (non-reactive, ignores plate/sponge position).
 - Execution speed 0.5× of demo speed gives smoother behavior on quasi-static tasks (attributed to imperfect latency compensation) — qualitative.
Collection protocol (App. B + DataScalingLaws App. B): per scene — (optional) gripper width calibration → mapping video (scene first, then task-relevant motions) → repeated demos, one video per demo; randomize initial gripper pose and object poses widely; textured scenes; standardize strategy across demonstrators; include explicit recovery demos (dish washing).
Failure/limitations: authors: kinematic infeasibility filtered after the fact; SLAM needs texture; UMI slower than bare hand. Critical read: 20 trials per ablation (single task for each ablation); in-the-wild evaluated only in 2 environments; the 0% narrow-domain baseline shows that fine-tuning a big pretrained encoder alone gives NO scene generalization — diversity must come from data (or augmentation).
Conflicts: Relative-to-chunk-start actions > delta > absolute agrees with our PROGRESS "chunk-wise delta" choice and the ActionSpaceStudy; disagrees with ACT (absolute better) — but UMI's absolute baseline suffers from SLAM-to-robot calibration error, which does NOT exist on a leader/follower SO-101 where absolute joint targets are exact; so the UMI result argues for relative actions mainly when the absolute frame is noisy (our camera-shift problem is on observations, not actions). Narrow-data 0% OOD agrees with DataScalingLaws and our SmolVLA observations (breaks on new lighting/camera). Temporal ensembling not used; latency matching instead — agrees with RTC/Legato style of explicit latency handling.
Relevance: HIGH for Q10/Q11/Q13, MEDIUM for Q05. (1) Our SmolVLA's jerky chunk boundaries with async inference are exactly UMI's "no latency matching" failure: measure camera latency (QR-code method), servo execution latency (sinusoid cross-correlation), and discard actions whose timestamps are already past at execution. (2) Wrist camera FoV matters: a narrow wrist cam hurts (55% vs 100%); choose a wide-angle wrist camera for SO-101. (3) Their 10 Hz is enough for quasi-static pick-place — our 10 Hz data is acceptable if latency is compensated, though ACT favors higher. (4) Collection: ~250–300 demos in ONE scene suffice for in-domain; OOD scene generalization required 1400 demos over 30 scenes — our 100–150 demos will not reach that from data alone → augmentation + deliberate condition variation. (5) The photometric augmentation recipe (crop 0.95, ±5° rotation, colour jitter incl. hue 0.08) is a reasonable default.
Decision impact:
 - Q13 diversity: in-the-wild diverse data 71.7% vs narrow-domain 0% in unseen envs with the same pretrained ViT-L — SUPPORTED "vary scene conditions during collection" — confidence H (consistent with DataScalingLaws).
 - Q13 recovery data: explicit recovery demos included for re-dirtied plate; policy resumes washing — SUPPORTED (qualitative) — confidence L.
 - Q13 demo count: ~250–300 demos per in-domain task used for 70–100% — informative upper bound vs our 50–150 — confidence M.
 - Q02 action space: relative trajectory to chunk-start pose 20/20 > step-delta 16/20 > absolute 5/20 (calibration-noisy) — SUPPORTED chunk-wise relative — confidence M (one task, 20 trials; absolute penalty partly calibration artefact).
 - Q10 latency: latency matching 87.5% vs 57.5% and removes jitter; 0.5× execution speed smoother — SUPPORTED explicit latency compensation — confidence M/H.
 - Q11 cameras: wide-FoV wrist cam essential (69° crop 55% vs fisheye 100%); wrist-only policy transfers across two robots — SUPPORTED wide wrist cam — confidence M.
 - Q03 vision: pretrained CLIP ViT fine-tuned at 10× lower LR ≫ scratch ResNet-34 (14/20 vs 0/10) — SUPPORTED — confidence M.
 - Q05 augmentation: standard crop/rotate/colour-jitter used by default; no ablation — ~ — confidence L.
 - Q14 robustness: pretrained encoder alone gives no scene robustness without diverse data (0%) — SUPPORTED — confidence M/H.
