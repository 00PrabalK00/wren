# RoboAgent-MT-ACT — RoboAgent: Generalization and Efficiency in Robot Manipulation via Semantic Augmentations and Action Chunking (2023, arXiv 2309.01918)
Setup: real Franka Panda + Robotiq w/ adaptive fingers, JOINT position control (8-D: 7 joints + gripper), 5 Hz(!), 4 RealSense D455 RGB (top/left/right + wrist). 7,500 VR-teleop trajectories (RoboSet MT-ACT subset) over 2 months, 4 physical kitchen setups, 12 skills, 38 tasks, 6 activities; ~200 trajectories/task on average (pick/place ≈40% of data). Policy MT-ACT: ACT (CVAE + transformer, 4 enc / 7 dec layers, ff 3200, 8 heads) + FiLM language conditioning (frozen pre-trained sentence encoder, 384-D), chunk H=20 (= 4 s at 5 Hz), temporal aggregation; lr 1e-5, bs 8, 2000 epochs, ~48 h on one 2080Ti for the augmented dataset. Eval: per activity/level ~10 trials (per-task success in 10% steps); robustness analysis 20 evals.
Method (semantic augmentation, offline, automatic): per frame, robot mask via forward kinematics; EE position prompts SAM to mask the interaction object → text-prompted inpainting of the object, kept consistent over time with Track-Anything; background: SAM picks random non-robot, non-target segments and inpaints them. Multiplies each trajectory by N augmentations per frame.
Claim: semantic augmentation + action chunking/CVAE let a single multi-task agent generalise with a small (7.5k) dataset.
Evidence:
 - Generalisation levels (L1 = pose + LIGHTING variation; L2 = new background/distractors; L3 = new object-skill combos; L4 = new kitchen). Augmentation gain relative to MT-ACT without aug: L1 ≈ +30% relative, L2 ≈ +100% relative, L3 ≈ +400% relative (text; bar values figure only).
 - L4 new kitchen, 3 tasks: MT-ACT 25% avg; all baselines 0; MT-ACT WITHOUT augmentation also 0.
 - VIL and RT-1-like trained on the same small dataset "completely fail" on L2 and L3; BeT (action clustering) significantly worse; per-step (non-chunked) methods weaker even at L1.
 - Universal policy per-task (Table 5): mostly 70–100%; hardest small/deformable grasps Pick Tea 40%, Pick Lid 50%.
 - Robustness: manual perturbation + blocking 1–3 of 4 cameras → ~70% of 20 evaluations succeed.
Ablations:
 - FiLM vs concatenation of language embedding: −5–10% without FiLM.
 - Chunk size 10/20/40 (at 5 Hz = 2/4/8 s): 20 best; 10 −5%; 40 >−20% (cannot correct errors).
 - Number of augmentations per frame: "strongly correlated" with performance, larger gains at L2/L3 — figure only, qualitative.
 - Single-task vs MT single-activity vs MT universal (Heat Soup): single-task ≈80% except Slide-In Bowl ≈20%; MT single-activity beats single-task on most tasks; MT universal < MT single-activity on most tasks (negative transfer across unrelated skills) — figure only.
 - Plasticity: fine-tune on new task (50 demos × 4 aug/frame = 250 traj) + 1/10 of old data → learns new task without much forgetting; slightly better L2/L3 than single-task on augmented new-task data — figure only.
Failure/limitations: authors: single skills only, no language generalisation study. Critical read: all key numbers are bar charts with ~10 trials per cell; the "no augmentation" ablation is only for MT-ACT on the full multi-task data; lighting is folded into L1 so the lighting-specific effect of augmentation is not separable; inpainting per frame with tracking is compute-heavy (not quantified); 5 Hz control is slow; comparison baselines (RT-1, BeT) are re-implementations tuned on a small data budget.
Conflicts: Agrees with GreenScreenAug/RoboEngine that scene-level augmentation mainly buys robustness to background/distractor shift (L2) and little in-distribution (L1). L3 gains (new objects) are larger here than GenAug's object results suggest because the base policy here is weaker there. Chunk sweep agrees with ACT (moderate chunk best, very long chunk hurts). Multi-task negative transfer (universal < activity) is a caution vs "co-training always helps" (MobileALOHA) — difference: here the co-trained tasks are unrelated skills, MobileALOHA co-trains on related static-ALOHA tabletop data.
Relevance: MEDIUM-HIGH. ACT+FiLM on 4 cameras incl. wrist is architecturally close to our plan; augmentation pipeline (FK robot mask + SAM + track + inpaint) is automatic and applicable to our scene camera, but inpainting every frame on an 8 GB laptop is slow — random-texture background replacement using the same SAM/FK masks gets most of the L2 benefit more cheaply (GreenScreenAug/RoboEngine). The plasticity result is directly useful for adding a second object later: 50 demos + augmentation + 10% replay of old data. Camera-dropout robustness (70%) suggests training with random camera dropout is worth having for our 2-camera setup.
Decision impact:
 - Q05 augmentation: semantic (inpainting) scene + object augmentation — SUPPORTED for background/distractor and new-object shifts, small in-distribution gain — confidence M (real, many tasks, but figure-only, ~10 trials/cell).
 - Q05 augmentation: more augmented copies per frame → better OOD — SUPPORTED — confidence L/M.
 - Q06 chunking: ~4 s chunk best at 5 Hz, 8 s hurts >20% — SUPPORTED moderate chunk — confidence M.
 - Q08 language: FiLM > concatenation by 5–10% — SUPPORTED FiLM — confidence M.
 - Q13 data: second task later = ~50 new demos + augmentation + ~10% replay of old task data, without forgetting — SUPPORTED — confidence L (one task, figure only).
 - Q13 multi-task co-training: unrelated-skill co-training can cause negative transfer (universal < single-activity) — WEAKENS indiscriminate co-training — confidence L.
 - Q11 cameras: multi-view + wrist gives robustness to blocking 1–3 cameras (~70% of 20) — SUPPORTED — confidence L.
