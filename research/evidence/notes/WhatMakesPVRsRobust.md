# WhatMakesPVRsRobust — What Makes Pre-Trained Visual Representations Successful for Robust Manipulation? (2023, arXiv 2312.12444)
Setup: 15 pretrained encoders (ResNet-50 and ViT-S/B; supervised ImageNet, Stylized-ImageNet ViT, DINO RN/ViT-S, MoCo-v3 RN/ViT-S/ViT-B, MAE-IN ViT-S, MVP ViT-B, MVP ViT-S(HOI), R3M, VIP, CLIP ViT-B/16, DINOv2 ViT-S/14). FROZEN encoder + 2-layer MLP, MSE BC, 10 demos (the R3M demos), lr 1e-3, 20k steps, proprio included. 10 sim tasks (FrankaKitchen 5, MetaWorld 5), 2 camera angles (a policy is trained per angle), 3 seeds → 60 policies per model × 11 eval conditions; ~9,000 evaluation runs in total. Shifts are zero-shot: lighting (cast left/right, dark, bright), object/scene texture (wood, metal, tile…), distractors (1/3/9 YCB objects). Real: ALOHA, ACT with a ViT-B encoder swapped in (4 cameras, 50 demos, lr 1e-5, chunk 100), screwdriver pickup, test with a moved object AND changed lighting direction, 10 rollouts.
Claim: manipulation-specific PVRs (R3M, MVP, VIP) do not generalize better OOD than standard ImageNet models; among ViTs, emergent segmentation (Jaccard of CLS attention on PASCAL VOC) predicts OOD success better than ImageNet linear-probe accuracy, shape bias or in-domain success.
Evidence (most per-model numbers are figure only):
 - In-distribution: R3M and VIP "comfortably beat" the standard baselines (figure only). Under shifts, the best model designed for manipulation ranks 7th of 15 on OOD average (figure only).
 - ViT-DINO (ViT-S) outperforms all three manipulation models under every shift except MetaWorld-distractors; MoCo-v3 ViT-B is also strong OOD; supervised ViT (ViT-INSUP) beats R3M and MVP in both environments; Stylized-ImageNet supervised ViT beats MAE-IN in- and out-of-distribution.
 - Architecture: 6 of the 7 best OOD models are ViTs. With matched data/recipe, ViT >> ResNet for DINO and supervised ImageNet; ≈ for MoCo-v3.
 - Correlations (figure only): Jaccard index vs OOD for ViTs is strong (R² and Spearman ρ high); shape bias correlates for ResNets only; ImageNet probe accuracy is a poor predictor for ViTs. Example outlier: MVP ViT-S(HOI) drops from 39.86 in-distribution to 6.63 OOD.
 - Distractors: Fig. 1 example: with segmenting features the drop is 37% vs 85% without; high-Jaccard ViTs keep the highest success as distractors go from 1 → 9.
 - DINOv2 ViT-S/14 (appendix): "surprisingly" WORSE in- and out-of-domain than DINO v1 despite being distilled from a bigger model on more data.
 - Real ALOHA screwdriver pickup, lighting + position shift: MoCo-v3 ViT-B 4/10 vs MVP ViT-B 0/10 (MVP fails to localize; MoCo localizes but misses the grasp point).
Ablations: augmentation strength during pretraining (DINO/MoCo multi-crop + color jitter + blur + solarization vs MAE crop only) is argued to matter more than supervision vs self-supervision. Appendix A.10: FINE-TUNING (25 demos, FrankaKitchen) still favours models not designed for manipulation (figure only).
Failure/limitations: authors: frozen protocol; sim-only correlations. My read: 10 demos + MLP on a frozen global/CLS feature is a narrow regime; camera-viewpoint shift is NOT tested (policies are trained per camera angle); the real test has 10 rollouts and 2 models only (it is also unclear whether the encoder was frozen or fine-tuned in ACT — lr 1e-5 suggests fine-tuned); many key numbers are only in figures. The Jaccard metric uses CLS attention, which is DINO-favourable by construction.
Conflicts: explains the iDP3/DP tension in part: in-distribution rankings (where R3M, fine-tuned encoders win) do NOT predict OOD rankings — so DP's "fine-tuned best" and iDP3's "fine-tuned R3M collapses OOD" measure different things. Agrees with UnbiasedLook (ImageNet-trained > Ego4D-trained on real, novel objects) and SPA (DINOv2 not top as frozen). Disagrees mildly with R3M's universal claims. DINOv2 < DINO v1 here mirrors SPA's DINOv2 mid-pack result, while RoboMP/RoboEngine still use DINOv2 successfully when fine-tuned with augmentation.
Relevance: our failures (lighting, different-looking pumpkin, distractors) are exactly these shifts. Evidence-based pick: a ViT with strong emergent segmentation, trained with heavy photometric augmentation (DINO v1 ViT-S/B, MoCo-v3 ViT-B, or a distilled model like Theia), rather than R3M/MVP/VC-1. ViT-S/B fits 8 GB. Camera shift remains uncovered.
Decision impact:
 - Q03 vision: for OOD robustness prefer ImageNet-SSL ViTs with emergent segmentation (DINO/MoCo-v3) over manipulation PVRs (R3M/MVP/VIP) — supported — M (9,000 sim evals, frozen, 10 demos; 1 small real test).
 - Q03 vision: ViT > ResNet for OOD with matched pretraining — supported — M.
 - Q03 vision: DINOv2-S worse than DINO v1 (frozen) — weakens a plain frozen DINOv2 — L/M (appendix remark, figure only).
 - Q14 robustness: in-distribution success is not a reliable proxy for OOD; evaluate under lighting/texture/distractor shift explicitly — supported — M/H.
 - Q05 augmentation: heavy photometric aug in PRETRAINING correlates with OOD robustness — supports strong photometric aug — L/M (correlational).
