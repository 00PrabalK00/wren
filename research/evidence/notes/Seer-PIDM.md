# Seer-PIDM — Predictive Inverse Dynamics Models are Scalable Learners for Robotic Manipulation (Seer) (2024, arXiv 2412.15109)
Setup: sim LIBERO-LONG (10 tasks × 50 demos; pretrain on LIBERO-90) and CALVIN ABC→D (pretrain on unlabeled play, fine-tune on annotated part); real Franka FR3 + Robotiq, two RealSense D435i (eye-on-hand wrist + eye-on-base), 15 Hz, 100 demos/task, 6 tasks (4 generalization-centric, 2 precision), pretrain on DROID (76k traj). Inputs 224×224, both views; frozen MAE ViT-B vision + CLIP ViT-B/32 text (251M frozen), Perceiver resampler, 24-layer GPT-2 backbone (hidden 384), 65M trainable (Seer-Large 315M). OBSERVATION HISTORY 7 frames (LIBERO & real) / 10 (CALVIN); action chunk 3; foresight n=3 steps. Losses: Lfore (MAE-style pixel MSE reconstruction of future RGB t+3 for both views from a [FRS] token + mask tokens; α=0.5) + Linv (Smooth-L1 arm + BCE gripper from [INV] tokens that attend to [FRS]). Compute: 8×4090; pretrain 30–40 h, fine-tune 6–24 h (sim). Eval: sim = avg of top-3 checkpoints; real = 15 cases × 3 trials, fixed 17th fine-tuned checkpoint.
Claim: end-to-end predictive inverse dynamics (predict future frame latent, then actions conditioned on it) is a scalable learner; pretraining on robot video+actions boosts sim and real success and robustness.
Evidence:
 - LIBERO-LONG avg success (Table 1): MT-ACT 41.0, MVP 68.2, MPI 77.3, OpenVLA 54.0, Seer scratch 78.7, Seer pretrained 87.7.
 - CALVIN ABC→D avg len (Table 2): GR-1 3.06, 3D Diffuser Actor 3.27, CLOVER 3.53, Seer scratch 3.64, Seer 3.98, Seer-Large scratch 3.83, Seer-Large 4.28.
 - Real (Table 4, 100 demos/task, SR% avg over 4 tasks): MVP 55.0, MPI 48.4, OpenVLA (7B full FT) 16.7, Seer scratch 60.0, Seer (DROID) 78.4. Per task Seer scratch→pretrained: Flip bowl 60.0→86.7, Stack cups 46.7→60.0, Wipe 60.0→73.3, Pick-place-close 73.3→86.7.
 - Precision (Table A-III): Press button MVP 46.7 / scratch 40.0 / Seer 60.0; Insertion 26.7 / 40.0 / 60.0.
 - Robustness (Fig 5, scratch → pretrained, SR%): distractor same-shape bowls 33.3→60.0; natural background (backdrop removed) 6.67→33.3; novel objects 46.7→60.0; extra strong light 46.7→66.7. Note scratch collapses under background change.
 - Cross-embodiment pretraining (Table A-IV, 6-task avg SR/score): scratch 53.3/26.7, OXE (no Franka) 56.7/26.8, DROID (same robot) 71.1/31.3 → pretraining only helps much when same embodiment + wrist views.
Ablations (Table 3, CALVIN ABC→D avg len):
 - Fine-tuning objectives from scratch: plain BC (no Lfore, no Linv conditioning) 3.31; + future-image prediction as side objective 3.41; + inverse dynamics conditioned on foresight 3.64 → aux future prediction +0.10, coupling actions to foresight +0.23 more.
 - Pre-training objectives: none 3.64; pretrain Lfore only 3.73; pretrain both 3.98.
 - Data efficiency (Fig 3, figure only): at 10% of downstream data, pretraining gives +187% relative success on LIBERO-LONG (≈5 demos/task) and +150% relative avg len on CALVIN vs scratch.
 - Model size (Fig 3, figure only): 65M → 107M → 316M trainable improves CALVIN avg len with or without pretraining (sim, lots of data).
Failure/limitations: authors — only 6 real tasks, one embodiment. My read: history of 7–10 frames is used but NEVER ablated, so no evidence on history. Pixel-reconstruction target (vs FLARE latent). Sim numbers are top-3-checkpoint averages (optimistic). OpenVLA baseline was chosen by lowest val loss and done badly (16.7%) — possibly partly a checkpoint-selection artifact. The +0.10 aux-only gain is on CALVIN full data (~20k annotated windows), not 50 demos. 316M total params with frozen ViT-B at 224² per view ×2 views × 7 frames is heavy for 8 GB inference (they use bf16).
Conflicts: agrees with MDT/FLARE/VPP that future prediction helps; unlike FLARE it reconstructs pixels and still gains, but its biggest gains come from pretraining on DROID, not from the aux loss itself. Its use of long history contrasts with MDT/BAKU (current obs only) — no ablation to settle it. Agrees with robomimic/Octo-era findings that bigger OpenVLA full-FT overfits at 100 demos.
Relevance: Closest real-robot analog to our setup among aux-loss papers: wrist + base RealSense, 100 demos/task, 15 Hz, generalization tests including lighting and background. Key transferable points: (1) at 100 demos, scratch Seer (65M trainable, frozen MAE ViT-B) gets 60% avg but collapses under background change (6.7%) — robustness came from DROID pretraining; (2) future-prediction aux loss alone gives a modest gain; conditioning actions on predicted future gives more; (3) evaluation at a fixed late checkpoint, not by val loss. For SO-101 there is no DROID-scale same-embodiment corpus (though LeRobot SO-100/101 community data exists).
Decision impact:
 - Q09 aux objectives: supports future-frame prediction (+0.10 avg len as side loss) and more strongly predictive-inverse-dynamics coupling (+0.33 total, 3.31→3.64 from scratch) — confidence M (sim, full data, single ablation).
 - Q09 / Q13 pretraining: supports pretraining the whole policy (vision+action) on same-embodiment robot data: real 60.0→78.4; OXE (other embodiments) only 53.3→56.7 — confidence M.
 - Q14 robustness: from-scratch 100-demo policy fails background change (6.7%) and suffers under light (46.7%); pretraining helps (33.3, 66.7) — confidence M (15×3 trials per condition).
 - Q07 history: uses 7–10 frames but no ablation — no evidence — n/a.
 - Q12 model size: bigger is better in sim with pretraining (65→316M) but OpenVLA-7B full-FT fails at 100 demos (16.7%) — confidence L/M.
 - eval/checkpoint: authors evaluate a fixed late checkpoint (real) / top-3 (sim); OpenVLA picked by lowest val loss performed worst — confidence L.
