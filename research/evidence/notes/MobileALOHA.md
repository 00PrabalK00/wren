# MobileALOHA — Mobile ALOHA: Learning Bimanual Mobile Manipulation with Low-Cost Whole-Body Teleoperation (2024, arXiv 2401.02117)
Setup: ALOHA arms (ViperX/WidowX leader-follower, 14-D absolute joint targets incl. grippers) on AgileX Tracer base (2-D velocity actions); 3 Logitech C922x RGB webcams (2 wrist + 1 front), 480×640, 50 Hz; ALL compute (collection + inference) on a consumer laptop RTX 3070 Ti 8 GB. 7 tasks, 50 in-domain demos per task (20 for Cook Shrimp and High Five), 2 expert operators. Policies: ACT (ResNet18 ImageNet, chunk 45, β=10, lr 2e-5, bs 16), Diffusion Policy (DDIM 50/10 steps, chunk 64, RandomCrop 0.95 + ColorJitter + ±5° rotation), VINN+chunking. Eval: 20 trials per task (5 for Cook Shrimp), sub-task success chained.
Method (co-training): mix each task's Mobile ALOHA data 50/50 per batch with the 825-episode static ALOHA dataset (disjoint tasks: ziploc, battery, tape, etc.; black tabletop, arms facing each other, different camera layout). Base actions zero-padded for static data; front cam dropped so both have 3 cams; action normalization from the in-domain (mobile) data only; no other processing.
Claim: co-training with a related-but-different static dataset boosts success and data efficiency of 50-demo imitation, across ACT/DP.
Evidence (ACT, whole-task success, co-train vs no co-train, 20 trials):
 - Wipe Wine 95 vs 50; Cook Shrimp 40 vs 20 (5 trials); Rinse Pan 80 vs 0; Use Cabinet 85 vs 85; Call Elevator 95 vs 0; Push Chairs 80 vs 0; High Five 85 vs 85.
 - Sub-task view: gains concentrate where precision/compounding error bottlenecks (Press Button, Turn On Faucet, Flip Shrimp); e.g. Wipe Wine "lift glass and wipe" 95 vs 58.
 - OOD extrapolation (Push Chairs, trained only on chairs 1–3): 4th chair 85 vs 70, 5th chair 89 vs 0; co-trained Wipe Wine policy better at the edge of the glass randomization region → co-training acts as regularizer against overfitting.
 - Other methods (Table 2): Diffusion Policy Wipe Wine 65 vs 35, Push Chairs 100 vs 80; VINN+chunk Wipe Wine 15 vs 20 (co-training hurt), Push Chairs 60 vs 40. DP < ACT on Wipe Wine at 50 demos ("50 demos not enough for diffusion given its expressiveness").
 - Open-loop replay of a demo gets 0% on every task (base replay error >10 cm on a 180° turn) → closed-loop visual correction essential.
Ablations:
 - Data efficiency (Wipe Wine, ACT, 25/35/50 demos): co-training consistently better; 35 co-trained demos 70% > 50 non-co-trained demos 50%.
 - Mixture ratio of static data 30/50/70%: 95/95/90 → insensitive.
 - Co-train vs pre-train-then-fine-tune on static data: co-train 95; pre-train no better than training on task data only (reported 40 vs 50) → forgetting during fine-tuning.
 - User study: novice teleop time drops 46→28 s (Wipe Wine) and 75→36 s (Use Cabinet) within 5 trials — operators reach expert speed quickly.
Failure/limitations: Cook Shrimp 40% (long horizon, 20 demos, white bowl on white table → low-contrast perception); VINN jerky at chunk switches; demos from 2 experts only; single-task. Critical read: 20 trials per cell; the co-training dataset shares the exact arm hardware, wrist-camera placement and action space — it is same-embodiment data; "no co-train" 0% on 3 tasks is suspicious of overfitting/hyperparameters rather than a smooth effect; no lighting/background shift test.
Conflicts: RoboAgent finds unrelated-skill multi-task training can cause negative transfer (universal < single-activity); here co-training on disjoint tasks helps — difference: here co-training data is a fixed 50% auxiliary stream with in-domain normalization and the target task is evaluated alone; RoboAgent's comparison was universal vs activity-level, both multi-task. DP weaker than ACT at 50 demos agrees with ACT/BAKU notes on overfitting of expressive heads in low data; contrasts with DP paper's strong results at ≥100–200 demos.
Relevance: HIGH. Same hardware class (leader-follower, absolute joint targets, wrist cams), same GPU budget (8 GB laptop), same demo budget (50). Directly usable: co-train our ~100 pumpkin demos 50/50 with public SO-100/SO-101 LeRobot datasets (same embodiment, joint space) — zero-pad/mask unshared dims, normalise with our own stats. Likely gains: precision sub-tasks (grasp), robustness at the edges of the placement range, fewer demos needed. Caveat: community SO-100 datasets differ in camera placement and calibration; mask the scene camera or treat as missing if views differ badly. Prefer co-training over pre-train-then-fine-tune.
Decision impact:
 - Q13 co-training: 50/50 co-training with same-embodiment off-task data improves success (+0 to +95 pts across 7 tasks) and data efficiency (35 co-trained > 50 plain) — SUPPORTED — confidence M/H (real, 7 tasks, 2 methods, 20 trials each).
 - Q13 co-training mix ratio: 30–70% insensitive — SUPPORTED — confidence M.
 - Q13 pre-train then fine-tune on off-task data: no gain vs co-train — WEAKENS sequential pretraining on small related data — confidence L/M (one task).
 - Q13 demo count: 50 demos per task sufficient for 80–95% in-distribution on hard mobile tasks with ACT + co-training; 20 demos for a 75 s task only 40% — SUPPORTED 50+ demos — confidence M.
 - Q01 action head: ACT (CVAE) > DP at 50 demos on precision task (95 vs 65 co-trained) — SUPPORTED lighter/CVAE-style head in low data — confidence L/M.
 - Q12 model size / compute: ACT trains and runs real-time on an 8 GB laptop GPU — SUPPORTED — confidence H.
 - Q14 robustness: co-training improves extrapolation at workspace edges (5th chair 89 vs 0) — SUPPORTED — confidence L/M.
