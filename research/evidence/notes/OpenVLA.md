# OpenVLA — OpenVLA: An Open-Source Vision-Language-Action Model (2024, arXiv 2406.09246)
Setup: Prismatic-7B VLM (SigLIP + DINOv2 fused ~600M vision encoder, 2-layer MLP projector, Llama-2 7B) fine-tuned to output actions as tokens: each dim discretized into 256 bins over the 1st–99th percentile, overwriting 256 least-used Llama tokens; next-token CE. Single 224x224 3rd-person image, no proprio, no history, no chunking, delta-EEF. Pretrain: 970k OXE trajectories (Octo mixture weights, DROID tried at 10% then removed), 27 epochs, 64 A100 × 14 days (21.5k A100-h), batch 2048, fixed LR 2e-5. Inference: 15 GB in bf16, ~6 Hz on RTX 4090. Eval: WidowX Bridge 170 rollouts (17 tasks × 10), Google robot 60 rollouts; fine-tune: Franka-Tabletop (5 Hz) and Franka-DROID (15 Hz), 7 tasks with 10–150 demos, 129 rollouts/approach.
Claim: an open 7B VLA beats RT-2-X (55B) by 16.5% absolute across 29 tasks and, fine-tuned, beats Diffusion Policy from scratch by 20.4% on average, particularly on multi-object language-grounded tasks; LoRA + 4-bit make it accessible.
Evidence:
 - Bridge zero-shot overall (Fig. 3 labels): RT-1-X 18.5, Octo 20.0, RT-2-X 50.6, OpenVLA 70.6 (bar labels; per-category figure only). Google robot: OpenVLA ≈ RT-2-X, both >> RT-1-X/Octo (figure only).
 - Fine-tuning (Fig. 5, per-bar numbers unreadable from text): DP (scratch) and DP-matched competitive or better on narrow single-instruction tasks ("Put Carrot in Bowl", "Pour Corn into Pot"); Octo/OpenVLA better on diverse multi-instruction + distractor tasks; OpenVLA only method ≥50% on every task; OpenVLA(scratch = Prismatic w/o OXE pretraining) lower on diverse tasks. DP produces "smoother and more precise trajectories" on dexterous narrow tasks.
 - PEFT (Table 1, 33 rollouts, Franka-Tabletop; smaller SigLIP-only OpenVLA variant): Full FT 69.7±7.2% (7188M params, 163 GB); last layer only 30.3±6.1%; frozen vision 47.0±6.9%; sandwich 62.1±7.9%; LoRA r=32 68.2±7.5% (97.6M, 59.7 GB); LoRA r=64 68.2±7.8%. LoRA ≈ 10–15 h on one A100.
 - Quantization (Table 2, 80 rollouts Bridge): bf16 71.3±4.8% (16.8 GB); int8 58.1±5.1% (10.2 GB; loss attributed to slow 1.2 Hz inference on A5000, not accuracy); int4 71.9±4.7% (7.0 GB; 3 Hz on A5000).
Ablations (Bridge small-scale, mostly qualitative):
 - VLM backbone: LLaVA +35% abs over IDEFICS-1 on language-grounding tasks; Prismatic (SigLIP+DINOv2) ~+10% over LLaVA on both single- and multi-object tasks.
 - Resolution 224 vs 384: no difference, 384 is 3x slower to train.
 - Vision encoder: fine-tuning it "crucial" (frozen worse) — also in Table 1: frozen vision 47.0 vs full 69.7.
 - Epochs: performance increases until train action-token accuracy >95% (27 epochs).
 - LR: 2e-5 best, warmup no benefit.
 - Inference speed matters: int8 slowdown to 1.2 Hz vs 5 Hz training dynamics dropped success 71→58.
Failure/limitations: Authors: single image only, no proprio/history; too slow for 50 Hz setups (needs chunking/speculative decoding); <90% reliability; VLM size and co-training questions open. Critical read: fine-tune compute (8 A100 × 5–15 h per task; LoRA 60 GB VRAM) is far beyond 8 GB; RDT-1B reports OpenVLA fine-tunes on ALOHA bimanual failing to converge (~60% token accuracy, static/random behaviour, 0% on most tasks); CogACT's Realman fine-tune gives OpenVLA 12.1% avg. Fine-tuning baseline DP from scratch uses absolute Cartesian + chunking while VLA uses relative single-step, so comparisons conflate action representation.
Conflicts: "Fine-tune the vision encoder" (OpenVLA, 7B, big data) vs HPT/Octo frozen or shallow encoders working at 100 demos, and VC-1/other notes where FT hurts with tiny data — the regime differs (OpenVLA pretrains on 970k traj first). Discrete-token head works here at 7B but Octo found 256-bin discrete worst at 27M (18%) and OpenVLA-OFT later showed continuous + chunked +5–14% on LIBERO. DP > VLA on narrow single-task fine-tunes agrees with OFT ALOHA results (DP competitive at 20–45 demos).
Relevance: Not runnable for training on 8 GB (LoRA needs ~60 GB); int4 inference fits 7 GB but at ~3 Hz on an A5000 — worse than our current SmolVLA 2 s latency issue only marginally. Key transferable lesson: for our narrow single-task pumpkin pick-place with 50–100 demos, a from-scratch Diffusion Policy is at least as good as a fine-tuned 7B VLA and smoother; VLA pretraining pays mainly for multi-object language grounding and distractors. Also: inference rate far below training control rate destroys performance (int8 case) — a direct warning for our 2 s latency.
Decision impact:
 - Q01 action head: discrete 256-bin single-step works only at 7B scale; DP smoother/more precise on narrow tasks — weakens discrete tokens for a small policy — confidence M.
 - Q03 vision encoder: frozen vision encoder 47.0 vs full FT 69.7 (7B VLA) — supports fine-tuning encoder when adapting a pretrained VLA — confidence M.
 - Q08 language: OXE/VLA pretraining helps mainly multi-instruction, multi-object tasks — confidence M.
 - Q10 latency: inference slowed to 1.2 Hz on 5 Hz task drops 71→58% — mismatch between inference and control rate is harmful — confidence M.
 - Q12 model size: 7B not needed for narrow single-instruction tasks (DP from scratch competitive) — confidence M.
 - Q14 robustness: VLA pretraining gives visual robustness to distractors/backgrounds (Bridge 70.6 vs Octo 20.0) — confidence M, but at 7B only.
