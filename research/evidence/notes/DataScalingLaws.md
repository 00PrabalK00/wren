# DataScalingLaws — Data Scaling Laws in Imitation Learning for Robotic Manipulation (2024, ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.18647)
Setup: data collected with UMI hand-held grippers (GoPro fisheye wrist cam, SLAM EE poses; ~90% of demos valid after SLAM filtering); deployed on Franka + WSG-50 on a movable table, single wrist GoPro, 224×224, 5 Hz env frequency. Policy: Diffusion Policy CNN U-Net (DDIM 16 steps) + DINOv2 ViT-L/14 fully fine-tuned (encoder LR 3e-5, head 3e-4), >396M params total, obs horizon 2 (3 with an extra 0.25–0.5 s older frame for Pour Water/Unplug), action horizon 16, temporal ensembling (8 steps, rate −0.01), bs 256, up to 75 h on 8×A800. Single-task policies. Tasks: Pour Water, Mouse Arrangement (main study), Fold Towels, Unplug Charger (verification). >40,000 demos total; 120 demos per env/object cell. Eval: tester-scored stages (0–3 pts per stage, normalized), ALWAYS in unseen environments and/or with unseen objects; blind randomized policy order, 8 envs/objects × 5 trials = 40 rollouts per policy; >15,000 rollouts overall.
Claim: generalization to unseen environments/objects follows a power law in the number of distinct training environments / objects / env-object pairs, NOT in the number of demos; diversity beats repetition.
Evidence:
 - Object generalization (32 objects, 1 env): score rises steeply; with 8 training objects both tasks >0.8, with 32 >0.9. More objects → fewer demos per object needed (Pour Water at 8 objects: 12.5% of demos lags 100% clearly; at 32 objects the gap nearly vanishes).
 - Environment generalization (32 envs, 1 object): monotone improvement with #envs; harder than object generalization (lower slope when few envs); 50% vs 100% of demos per env curves overlap → per-env demo count saturates fast.
 - Env+object pairs: largest gains; demo fraction curves 25% and 100% overlap → simultaneous diversity makes each demo worth more.
 - Power laws on optimality gap (1 − score) vs #pairs, e.g. Mouse Arrangement y = 1.263·x^−0.683 (r = 0.942 on log axes; exponent sign from fit); predicts 1,191 pairs for 0.99. Total demos vs score: only weak power law (r = −0.62 Pour, −0.79 Mouse).
 - Demo count at fixed diversity (M=16 envs, N=64 objects; raw table, series normalised by 9 and 6 — attribution of series to task inferred from table order): 64/100/200/400/800/1600/3200/6400 demos → 0.48/0.68/0.76/0.78/0.78/0.80/0.79/0.72 and 0.29/0.50/0.55/0.63/0.66/0.63/0.65/0.63 → plateau ≈400–800 demos.
 - Saturation by #pairs: 8/16/32 pairs plateau at 400/800/1600 total demos → recommendation K = 50 demos per env-object pair.
 - Multiple objects per env (Fig 6 heatmap, M ∈ {1..16}, N/M ∈ {1..4}): extra objects per env help when #envs is small, negligible at M=16 (heatmap values garbled in extraction — qualitative only).
 - Verification (32 env-object pairs × 50 demos, 8 unseen envs × 2 unseen objects × 5 trials; ONE afternoon, 4 collectors): success Pour Water 85.0 ± 19.4%, Mouse Arrangement 92.5 ± 9.7%, Fold Towels 87.5 ± 17.1%, Unplug Charger 90.0 ± 14.1%.
 - Controlling for total demos (App. G.2, re-plotted at ≈200…1600 demos): more envs/objects still better at equal demo count; ~1600 demos total saturates; ~100-demo level "unstable and unreliable" with many envs/objects.
Ablations (Pour Water, 32 pairs, 50% data):
 - Encoder training: fine-tuned DINOv2 ViT-L 0.90; LoRA r=8 0.72; frozen DINOv2 0.00; scratch ViT-L 0.03.
 - Encoder size: ViT-S 0.66, ViT-B 0.81, ViT-L 0.90.
 - Diffusion U-Net size: small 0.88, base 0.90, large 0.83 (action head capacity not a bottleneck).
 - Extra older history frame (0.25/0.5 s) "significantly improves" Pour Water/Unplug Charger (stage ambiguity) — no numbers.
 - Validation action MSE vs real score: sometimes r = −0.98, but fails elsewhere (MSE rises at 16 objects; LoRA MSE 0.0049 < full FT 0.006 yet score 0.72 < 0.90) → MSE only a debugging tool.
Collection protocol tips (App. B, UMI): randomize INITIAL GRIPPER POSE (height, orientation) every demo — otherwise the policy is sensitive to start pose; randomize object position as widely as kinematics allow; add textures/distractors (also acts as augmentation); standardize behaviour pattern and task duration across collectors to reduce multimodality; don't move distractors during demos; keep other moving things out of view.
Failure/limitations: authors: single-task only, DP only, UMI demos have small SLAM errors, 4 tasks. Critical read: regime is far bigger than ours (models ~400M params, 8×A800, ≥1,600 demos for the headline); scores are stage-scored not binary; frozen DINOv2 = 0.00 is extreme and may reflect this specific ViT-L + fisheye setup. The "50 demos per pair" rule was derived at 8–32 pairs; the ≤100-demo regime is explicitly called unstable. Env diversity here includes lighting/background/table changes in the wild — exactly our failure axes — but no separate lighting number.
Conflicts: Agrees with DemoGen (coverage > repetitions; saturation per configuration) and GreenScreenAug/RoboAgent (scene diversity drives OOD, whether real or augmented). Frozen DINOv2 ≈ 0 agrees with DP-style "fine-tune pretrained encoder" and contradicts frozen-feature policies; encoder scaling helps here (0.66→0.90) whereas BAKU warns large models overfit at 50 demos — difference is data (thousands of diverse demos vs 50 in one scene). Temporal ensembling used for smoothness here, whereas our PROGRESS plan drops it for RTC/Legato; both are chunk-boundary fixes.
Relevance: HIGH for Q13 — it is the only controlled study of diversity vs count. For one SO-101 in one lab we cannot get 32 environments, but "environment" can be approximated by deliberately varying lighting (lamps on/off, colour temperature, time of day), tablecloths/backgrounds, distractors, and small camera nudges between sessions; "object" by buying 4–8 different pumpkins (size/colour/texture). The result that 50% vs 100% demos per environment overlap means: 100 demos spread over ~8–10 conditions ≫ 100 demos in one condition. Fine-tuning (not freezing) the pretrained encoder is strongly supported; ViT-L is out of budget on 8 GB, so expect a ViT-S/B-size penalty (0.66/0.81 vs 0.90).
Decision impact:
 - Q13 diversity vs count: split demos across many scene conditions and object instances; ~5–15 demos per condition is fine once conditions are many — SUPPORTED — confidence H (>15k blind rollouts, 4 tasks).
 - Q13 demo count: per env-object pair ~50 demos saturates; total 400–800 demos plateau at 16×64 diversity; ≤100 total demos is noisy — SUPPORTED (sets expectation that 100–150 demos will give partial, not full, OOD robustness) — confidence M.
 - Q13 protocol: randomize start pose of the arm and object positions every demo, standardize strategy/speed — SUPPORTED — confidence M (practitioner advice, no ablation numbers).
 - Q14 robustness: real diversity of environments is the primary lever for lighting/background shift; object diversity is cheaper to learn (8 objects → >0.8) — SUPPORTED — confidence H.
 - Q03 vision: pretrained + FULL fine-tuning required (frozen 0.00, scratch 0.03, LoRA 0.72, full 0.90); bigger encoder better at large data — SUPPORTED fine-tuned pretrained encoder — confidence H.
 - Q12 model size: scaling action head does not help (0.88/0.90/0.83) — SUPPORTED small action head — confidence M.
 - Q07 history: one extra older frame helps stage-ambiguous tasks — weakly SUPPORTED — confidence L.
 - Evaluation: offline action MSE unreliable for model selection — SUPPORTED — confidence M.
