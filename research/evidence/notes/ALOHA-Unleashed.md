# ALOHA-Unleashed — ALOHA Unleashed: A Simple Recipe for Robot Dexterity (2024, arXiv 2410.13126)
Setup: ALOHA 2 bimanual (2× 6-DoF ViperX + grippers, 14-D ABSOLUTE joint targets), 4 RGB cams (incl. 2 wrist) at 480×640, 50 Hz; uncalibrated 10-robot fleet in 2 buildings. 5 real tasks, 26k+ teleop episodes from 35 non-expert operators over 8 months (≈3.2k–8.7k per task). Policy: per-task Transformer diffusion policy — 4 ImageNet ResNet50s (one per cam), 85M-param encoder, 55M-param denoiser, 217M total (Small variant 150M used for ablations); chunk 50 (1 s) executed OPEN-LOOP (no temporal ensembling), DDIM 50 steps, 0.043 s per inference on RTX 4090; trained 2M steps, bs 256, 64 TPUv5e (~265 h). Eval: 20 trials per task; sim 50 rollouts (3 seeds).
Claim: large-scale, protocol-driven teleop data + a transformer diffusion policy yields dexterous bimanual skills (shirt hanging, shoelace tying).
Evidence:
 - Real success (Table 1): ShirtEasy 75%, ShirtMessy 70% (8658 demos); LaceEasy 70%, LaceMessy 40% (5133); FingerReplace 75% (5247); GearInsert 1/2/3 gears 95/75/40% (4005); RandomKitchen Bowl 95%, +Cup 65%, +Fork 25% (3198 total, only 216 in-domain from the eval robot — rest from another building/hardware iteration).
 - Generalization observed qualitatively: unseen adult long-sleeve grey shirt and an unseen robot in a home building with white-wall background succeed; shirt init ±60° handled; 180°/face-down shirts, flipped shoes, tangled laces fail (never in data).
Ablations (Small 150M model):
 - Data quantity (Shirt, 100/75/50/25% = 8658/6493/4329/2164 demos): ShirtEasy 75/75/85/30%; ShirtMessy 70/70/20/0% → harder/more varied initial states need far more data; easy variant saturates early.
 - Data filtering by episode duration (from the 25% subset, ShirtEasy): all 2164 episodes 30%; shortest 75% (1623) 30%; shortest 50% (1082) 55%; shortest 25% (541, mostly mistake-free) 40% → moderate filtering of slow/mistake-laden demos helps, over-filtering hurts; authors: some suboptimal data carries useful recovery/retry behaviour.
 - Diffusion vs L1 regression (ACT-like, same ~150M transformer): ShirtMessy real 70% vs 25%. Sim (DP Small / DP XS-LowRes / ACT XS-LowRes): SingleInsertion 72 / 58±3 / 32 (522 demos); DoubleInsertion 60 / 48±2 / 58 (201 demos); MugOnPlate 80 / 74±0 / 40 (550 demos).
 - Chunk 10 vs 50 (sim SingleInsertion): best single seed 66 vs 58±3 → task-dependent; real uses 50.
 - DDIM 50/25/2 inference steps: little effect in sim (figure only).
 - Authors' practice note: ACT with a smaller network is easier to tune and often better in LOW data; they use ACT for "signs of life" then switch to diffusion once large multi-operator multimodal data exist.
Failure/limitations: authors: one task per model, 1 s open-loop replanning too slow for reactive tasks, many demos needed. Critical read: data regime (thousands of demos per task, 217M params, 64 TPUs) is 30–80× ours; only 20 trials; filtering result from a single seed/task; "generalization" results are anecdotal (no rates).
Conflicts: Diffusion ≫ L1 at thousands of multimodal, multi-operator demos agrees with ACT's own CVAE ablation (plain L1 collapses on human multimodal data) and BAKU/our plan to use a generative head; but at low data the same authors say ACT is easier — consistent with MobileALOHA (ACT 95 > DP 65 at 50 demos). Open-loop 1 s chunks with no ensembling contrasts with ACT's temporal ensembling and DataScalingLaws' use of it — they had 50 Hz and high-quality data; our 10 Hz SO-101 case differs. Recovery-from-suboptimal-data point agrees with DemoGen ADR (disturbance data needed) and SO101-Benchmark (recovery weak).
Relevance: MEDIUM. Not our data regime, but three transferable lessons for a 50–150 demo SO-101 dataset: (1) write a short collection PROTOCOL (fixed strategy, start pose, reset procedure) and do 5–10 test episodes before the real run, even for a single operator; (2) filter out the slowest/messiest ~25–50% only if we have a surplus — at ~100 demos, prefer to keep them unless they contain clear mistakes that were NOT corrected; (3) policies fail on initial states never demonstrated (face-down shirt ≈ pumpkin lying on side/in a corner) → explicitly cover such states. Open-loop 1 s chunks are fine at 50 Hz with smooth diffusion outputs; hardware variance across an uncalibrated fleet is absorbed by visual relative control, which suggests wrist camera helps absorb small calibration drift.
Decision impact:
 - Q13 data quality: moderate filtering of long/mistake-laden demos helps (30→55%), aggressive filtering hurts (40%) — SUPPORTED "keep some imperfect demos with corrections" — confidence L/M (single task, 20 trials).
 - Q13 recovery data: recovery/retry behaviours emerge only when present in data; unseen failure states (flipped objects) are not recovered — SUPPORTED explicit recovery demos — confidence M (qualitative, consistent with DemoGen/SO101-Benchmark).
 - Q13 demo count: harder init distributions need far more data (ShirtMessy 0% at 2164 demos vs 70% at 6493) — SUPPORTED "narrow init distribution first, widen with more data" — confidence M.
 - Q01 action head: diffusion ≫ L1 regression at large multimodal data (70 vs 25 real); ACT preferred by authors at low data — SUPPORTED generative head, data-dependent — confidence M.
 - Q06 chunking: 1 s open-loop chunk without ensembling works at 50 Hz; chunk size task-dependent — ~ neutral — confidence L.
 - Q12 model size: 150–217M params used with thousands of demos only; not evidence for large models at 100 demos — ~ — confidence L.
