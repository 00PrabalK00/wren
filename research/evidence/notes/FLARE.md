# FLARE — FLARE: Robot Learning with Implicit World Modeling (2025, arXiv 2505.15659)
Setup: sim RoboCasa 24 tasks (Panda; left, right, wrist cams) and 24 GR-1 humanoid tabletop tasks (single egocentric head cam); real GR-1 humanoid, 4 pick-place tasks (≈100 traj/task post-training), plus 5 novel objects (1 or 10 teleop demos/object + 150 GoPro human egocentric videos/object). Policy: GR00T-N1-style flow-matching DiT (8 layers, alternating self/cross-attn), K=4 Euler steps, action chunk H=16; vision-language encoder = SigLIP2-Large (256×256) vision + text → 4 fusion transformer layers → Q-former to 32 tokens. FLARE: add M=32 learnable "future tokens" to the DiT input; at layer 6/8 project them with an MLP and maximize cosine similarity to the (EMA ρ=0.995) target embedding of the observation at t+H; λ=0.2. Compute: in-domain experiments 32 H100, batch 1024, 80k steps; embedding pretraining 256 H100, ~3,000 h cross-embodiment data. Eval: 50 episodes/task every 1000 steps, report MAX over final 5 checkpoints (sim); final checkpoint (real).
Claim: aligning intermediate policy features with a compact latent embedding of the FUTURE observation (implicit world model, no pixel reconstruction) improves diffusion/flow policies and lets them learn from action-free human video.
Evidence (Table 1, in-domain embedding, same architecture/steps):
 - RoboCasa 24-task avg: FLARE 70.1 vs policy-only (same arch) 61.9 vs UWM (joint video-latent + action diffusion, 400k steps) 60.8 vs GR00T N1 scratch 60.6 vs Diffusion Policy 51.7. Pick-place subset: 53.2 / 43.8 / 35.6 / 44.1 / 29.2.
 - GR1 24-task avg: FLARE 55.0 vs policy-only 44.0 vs UWM 29.5 vs GR00T N1 scratch 45.1 vs DP 40.9.
 - Policy-only trained 160k steps: 44.1% (= 80k), so gain isn't from extra steps.
 - Post-training with cross-embodiment pretrained embedding (Fig 6, figure only; text): +10 pts on RoboCasa with 100 traj/task; with 1000 traj the (never-saw-RoboCasa) pretrained target gives 71.3 vs in-domain 70.2. Real GR-1: up to 95.1%, avg +14 pts over policy-only; qualitative — baseline knocks over can/bottle, FLARE maneuvers around.
 - Human video co-training (Fig 7): novel objects, 1 teleop traj/object: 37.5 (no human video) vs 60.0 (with); 10 traj/object: 42.5 vs 80.0.
Ablations:
 - Target embedding (Table 2, 24 GR1 tasks): no FLARE 43.9; raw SigLIP2 tokens of frame t+16 49.6; 2×2-pooled SigLIP2 50.9; action-aware embedding 55.0 → even a generic frozen encoder as target gives +6–7 pts.
 - EMA of target (Fig 9, RoboCasa 24×300 traj, figure values): baseline ≈60.1; ρ=0.99 ≈63.5; 0.995 ≈66.4 (best); 0.999 ≈65.7; 1.0 (frozen) ≈64.6 — all beat baseline (value-to-ρ mapping read from figure, approximate).
 - Alignment layer / λ (Fig 8, figure only): robust across range; aligning too early (layer 4 of 8) drops notably.
Failure/limitations: authors: mainly pick-place, real humanoid; still needs a few expert demos; controlled GoPro videos. My read: big-compute regime (32–256 H100s, SigLIP2-L ~300M+ encoder, batch 1024); sim results = max over last 5 checkpoints; single seed apparently; the headline multitask numbers use 24 tasks jointly (hundreds–thousands of demos total), not 50 demos single-task. Real eval: 8 initial frames/task — small.
Conflicts: agrees with MDT (future-prediction aux helps, full reconstruction unnecessary/worse) and argues against pixel/VAE-latent co-generation (UWM 60.8 even with 5× steps). Seer and VPP show explicit future image prediction also helps — difference is compute/latency: FLARE adds only 32 tokens at train time and nothing needed at inference (future tokens are in the forward pass but cheap).
Relevance: The recipe is portable to a small ACT/DP-style transformer: add K future tokens, cosine-align them to a frozen pretrained encoder's (DINOv2/SigLIP pooled) embedding of the frame at t+chunk. Zero extra inference cost, small train cost (one extra encoder forward on the future frame, can be precomputed if target frozen: ρ=1.0 still helped). Evidence at our data scale is thin: +10 pts at 100 traj/task was with a pretrained big encoder and multitask pooling. Human-video co-training is interesting but not our priority.
Decision impact:
 - Q09 aux objectives: supports latent future-embedding alignment (no pixels) as a cheap train-only aux loss — RoboCasa 61.9→70.1, GR1 44.0→55.0, generic SigLIP2 target +6–7 — confidence M (large-compute multitask sim; mechanism cheap to replicate).
 - Q09 aux objectives: weakens joint pixel/VAE-latent future generation for small policies (UWM 60.8/29.5 vs FLARE 70.1/55.0) — confidence M.
 - Q01 head: flow matching DiT (K=4) ≫ U-Net Diffusion Policy here (61.9 vs 51.7 policy-only) — confidence L/M (arch & encoder confounded).
 - Q13 data: action-free human video + 10 demos/object doubles novel-object success (42.5→80.0) — confidence L/M.
 - eval/checkpoint: authors report max over last 5 checkpoints (sim) — no evidence on val-loss correlation — n/a.
