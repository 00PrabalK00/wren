# Executive summary: what to build for the SO-101 pumpkin task

Sources: 480 full-text paper notes, 1,458 ledger findings, 3,133 abstract cards, and the two real-time reports in this directory. Every number below is quoted from a note; the file is given in brackets. Full detail, all evidence tables and conflict adjudications are in synthesis/FINAL_REPORT.md.

## 1. What went wrong, in the literature's words

- **Your 1.9 s SmolVLA latency is a software/power problem.** Others measure SmolVLA at 101 ms on a laptop RTX 5080 [realtime/notes/A2C2.md], 82 ms real [fulltext/notes/ReactVLA.md], and 20 ms when compiled [realtime/notes/FlashVLA.md]. Power-saver mode alone is 1.6× on the same chip [realtime/notes/JetsonPI.md]. eval_real.py runs fp32 without compile and only asks for the next chunk after the current one is used up, so the arm starves.
- **The three failures (night light, pink pumpkin, moved camera) are exactly the three shifts every small policy trained on one-scene data fails**, including SmolVLA on SO-101: 70→0% under a coloured spotlight without augmentation [wave4/notes/W4_LightsCameraMalfunctionWhenIlluminationR.md], 65.3→7.5% under a camera-view change even with a wrist camera [wave4/notes/W4_InfiNoVAInfiniteNovelViewAugmentationfor.md], 75/70→30/35% in a similar new room [wave4/notes/W4_SEVOSemanticEnhancedVirtualObservationfo.md]. Big pretrained VLAs are not immune (π0 63→16% at 15° camera rotation [wave4/notes/W4_FromFixedtoFreeCamerasCalibrationFreeVie.md]).
- **The jumpy async motion is the known "naive async + averaging" failure.** Fading between two independently sampled chunks averages two different plans; this caused oscillation and even protective stops elsewhere [fulltext/notes/RTC.md]; on an SO-101 with 50 demos, naive async scored 17/20 with oscillation and 20/20 once the head was conditioned on the committed prefix [realtime/notes/ActionControlNet.md].

## 2. The design (about 55M parameters, runs in ~15–30 ms on the RTX 5060, estimate)

1. **Eyes:** a pretrained DINO-family ViT-S image encoder, shared by the RealSense scene camera and the wrist camera, fine-tuned at a 10× lower learning rate with augmentation always on. Keep the 16×16 patch tokens (pooled to 64 per camera), never a single vector. Frozen features scored 0.00 vs 0.90 fine-tuned [fulltext/notes/DataScalingLaws.md]; pooled tokens 0.40 vs 1.00 [wave3/notes/W3_CageCausalAttentionEnablesDataEfficientG.md]; scratch CNNs have ~1–7% lighting robustness [wave4/notes/W4_MINERVAHowSmallCanaManipulationPolicyBea.md].
2. **Cameras:** keep both. Wrist is the anchor (never dropped); the scene view gets token dropout and stronger geometric augmentation because it is the view that overfits to lighting and camera pose (real pickup 75% with both, 7.5% without wrist, 20% without scene [wave4/notes/W4_MResTMultiResolutionSensingforRealTimeCo.md]).
3. **Brain:** a ~20M transformer over [scene tokens, wrist tokens, one joint-state token]. Bigger from-scratch models overfit at 50 demos (114M collapsed to 0.19 while 4–31M were all fine [wave3/notes/W3_BAKUAnEfficientTransformerforMultiTaskPo.md]).
4. **Hands:** a ~10M flow-matching action head predicting 1.6 s of absolute leader-joint targets (16 steps at 10 fps, 48 at 30 fps), 5–10 Euler steps, trained so it can continue a committed prefix (training-time RTC) and so it uses the robot state correctly under delay (VLASH offsets). Plain L1 collapsed on human demos 35.3→2% [fulltext/notes/ACT.md]; the flow head is also what makes the smooth-async tricks possible.
5. **State:** current joint positions only, with noise and dropout; no velocities, no history (history hurt robustness 89.7→84.4 [wave3/notes/W3_GeometricActionModelforRobotPolicyLearni.md]; state used as a shortcut gave 0/30 under a table-height change [wave4/notes/W4_DoYouNeedProprioceptiveStatesinVisuomoto.md]).
6. **Execution:** execute 0.5–0.8 s of each chunk, then use the next one; shorten near the grasp. Executing 1 step is stop-go; executing the whole chunk loses 20–30 points (Fix15 0.800 vs Fix40 0.533 [realtime/notes/DenoisingVarianceChunking.md]).
7. **Not in v1:** depth/point clouds (add an object-segmented branch later only if the new-pumpkin or moved-camera tests stay weak; full-scene point clouds collapse under clutter, 55.2→5.0 [wave3/notes/W3_RoboTwin20AScalableDataGeneratorandBench.md]) and language (add a frozen small text encoder + FiLM when the second object is introduced).

## 3. The data plan (the biggest lever)

Collect 120–150 demos at 30 fps, spread on purpose: 4–6 different pumpkins, 3–4 lighting conditions (including the night one), 3–4 backgrounds/table covers, distractor objects in ~30% of demos, the scene camera nudged a few cm between sessions (with a fiducial so it can be re-registered), placements on a grid covering slightly more than where you will test, a slightly randomised start pose. One operator, one consistent strategy, decisive motions, slow gripper closes. 15–25% of episodes include a deliberate missed grasp followed by a re-grasp. Trim idle frames at the start and end. Green screen behind the workspace is cheap and the best-evidenced background fix (+65% in new scenes [fulltext/notes/GreenScreenAug.md]); otherwise segment the scene stream offline once for background replacement. Diversity beats count: half the demos per condition gave the same result once conditions were many [fulltext/notes/DataScalingLaws.md]; on an SO-101, varied backgrounds and lighting in the demos moved a novel room from 30% to 85% [wave4/notes/W4_SEVOSemanticEnhancedVirtualObservationfo.md].

Training augmentation: random crop/shift and small perspective warps, photometric jitter with hue fixed (full hue jitter makes the policy colour-blind: 77.5→47.5 on a colour task [wave4/notes/W4_LightsCameraMalfunctionWhenIlluminationR.md]), background replacement, pumpkin-region colour randomisation, copy-paste distractors, scene-token dropout, state noise. Pick checkpoints by robot trials, not validation loss (lowest-val-loss checkpoints were 10–100% worse [fulltext/notes/robomimic.md]).

## 4. The real-time plan

- Stage 0: bf16, torch.compile/CUDA graphs, performance power mode; request the next chunk while the current one executes; measure camera latency (film a clock) and servo lag (command a sinusoid); target under 100 ms total, delay of 1–2 control steps at 10 Hz.
- Executor (your 100 Hz process): timestamped action buffer, discard stale actions, interpolate to the servo rate, hold last pose if starved; safety by slowing/pausing along the path, never by bending it.
- Continuity: prefix-conditioned head + state roll-forward; no fade, no temporal ensembling.
- Monitors: an inter-chunk disagreement score (0.2 ms, balanced accuracy 0.95 on a low-cost arm [realtime/notes/RewindIL.md]) plus an embedding-novelty score, thresholds calibrated on 10–30 successful runs; on alarm re-query, on persistent alarm rewind to the pre-grasp pose and retry (18.3→76.7% under perturbation [realtime/notes/RewindIL.md]).
- Evaluate with ≥20 trials per condition (50 for the decisions that matter [fulltext/notes/LBM-CarefulExamination.md]), conditions: in-distribution, lighting, new pumpkin, camera nudge, distractors, edge placements, perturbation; record recovery rate and a failure taxonomy.

## 5. The first three things to build

1. **Make the current stack honest (1–2 days):** fix eval_real.py (bf16, compile, overlapped requests, timestamped buffer, no blend), measure latencies, and run 20 in-distribution SmolVLA trials as the fixed baseline. Then train LeRobot ACT on the existing 51 episodes and run 20 trials in-distribution and 20 at night. This calibrates the rig and the evaluation before any new model.
2. **Collect dataset v2 with the protocol above and train ACT on it.** Compare ACT-on-v2 versus ACT-on-100-single-condition demos on the lighting and new-pumpkin cells. If diversity alone lifts those cells from ~0–30% to >60%, most of the problem is data and the rest is incremental.
3. **Implement the v1 policy as a LeRobot policy class** (DINO ViT-S + 20M trunk + flow head with prefix conditioning) and run the ablations in this order: encoder (DINO vs ResNet-18), head (flow vs L1), execution horizon, scene-token dropout, auxiliary losses. Keep whichever of v1 or ACT wins on the robustness cells; then add the async continuity, the monitors, and an intervention round.

## 6. Honest confidence

High: the latency diagnosis; diversity-first data; hue-fixed photometric aug plus background replacement; keep both cameras; absolute leader targets; no blending/ensembling with a stochastic head; execution horizon of ~0.5–1 s.
Medium: flow head over L1 at 100 demos (real evidence is split; ACT is the safe baseline); DINO-ViT-S fine-tuned over ResNet-18 (ResNet won at ≤25 demos in one study); scene-token dropout; auxiliary losses; monitors on this arm.
Low / thin: camera-shift robustness without re-registration (unsolved in the corpus); how much the new-pumpkin cell improves without an object-centric branch; 30 fps vs 10 fps on this arm; training a ViT-S at 224² on 8 GB (inferred, not measured); the gripper-load grasp check (our idea, untested).
